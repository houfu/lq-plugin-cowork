## Response documents and drafting routes

Choose the requested work product before the general discovery intake. Read the supplied files first; nothing here needs an approved objection library or a separate review application, and a matter intake is not a prerequisite for a response shell or a one-off draft.

- **Prepare an editable response shell:** read [response-shells.md](references/response-shells.md). Preserve exact served requests, adapt the supplied format, and leave useful expandable drafting areas. Use the bundled neutral RFP format in [assets/rfp-response-shell.docx](assets/rfp-response-shell.docx) only when no example is supplied. Copying selected existing language at the lawyer's direction does not require fresh legal research.
- **Draft or revise objections directly:** follow the substantive method below, and the response-shell formatting reference when a Word document is requested. Use the supplied language, context and instructions, and distinguish proposed positions from reported facts.
- **Reuse an approved bank of objection wording:** when the lawyer supplies one — a prior response set, a firm precedent file, an approved list of grounds — treat it as a source of starting language, never as approval for the current request. Split combined responses into individual objection grounds, consolidate semantically equivalent examples, and keep material scope differences as separate variants. For each current request, assess the plausible approved grounds across that bank and surface the reasonably apparent possibilities, including conditional ones, with their connections and missing inputs. Prior request numbers are source locators, not applicability restrictions. Leave every surfaced candidate unchosen: surfacing makes an option visible, the lawyer's selection is what puts wording into a draft, and approval for reuse decides neither suitability nor whether the position will be taken.
- **Assemble the lawyer's decisions into a response:** place the selected wording into the shell exactly as selected, without a second per-request approval of unchanged wording, and record the requests the lawyer reviewed with no objection. Requests marked as needing input stay open. Produce the editable Word file with the Word skill; the lawyer finishes substantive responses there.

Keep each delivery focused on the requested artifact and the few unresolved items that matter. Faithful capture and assembly of supplied wording claim no new legal analysis, and subsequent edits in Word are free drafting work.
