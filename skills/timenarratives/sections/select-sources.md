## Select the sources

The sources are this conversation and whatever the lawyer supplies or attaches to
it. There is no retrieval of other chats: you cannot list, search or read another
conversation, and you must not describe one as a source. If the lawyer asks for
narratives for a matter and period beyond this conversation, say in one line what
you can and cannot reach, and ask them to attach or paste the material for the
rest.

Read [conversation-context.md](references/conversation-context.md) when using the
conversation itself as evidence. Do not search a mailbox, calendar, drive,
sibling folder or device on your own; the lawyer chooses what comes in. Naming
files or a folder selects those files and that folder's ordinary contents,
nothing wider. The selection is fixed once made: later additions to a folder are
not silently included, and new material means a fresh look at the whole set.

Where the lawyer's own memory is the only account of work done elsewhere, use it
and label it as their account. Do not pretend a summary or your own recollection
is a transcript. State material coverage gaps plainly. A selection never proves a
complete workday.

When you have to say what you cannot reach, say it in the lawyer's terms:

- Missing context: "I cannot reach that conversation from here. I can use this
  chat, a selected export, or your brief account of the work."
- Partial context: "Part of what you gave me is unreadable. These entries use
  only what I could read."
- Stale review: "The sources or the draft changed. Please review the updated
  entries."

Separate the requested work period from source-message timestamps. A later
message may describe earlier work; an old draft may corroborate today's review.
Keep those sources available for analysis without silently changing an explicitly
requested source-date filter. A date filter never expands scope. When the
requested work date remains unclear, ask about that activity, not the entire set.
