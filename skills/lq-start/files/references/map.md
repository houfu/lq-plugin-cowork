# The map — every skill by the situation that calls for it

Read before naming any skill. Every entry says what the skill is for, what it
is not for, and the neighbour to use instead. That is the routing.

There are three LegalQuants bundles for Copilot Cowork:

- **LegalQuants litigation skills** — source-grounded workflows for litigators.
- **LegalQuants transactional skills** — contract and deal workflows.
- **The LegalQuants Companion** — your own journey with AI as a lawyer, rather
  than a matter.

Every entry below carries the bundle or bundles it ships in, in brackets after
the skill name. The first group is shared daily practice and ships in both
practice bundles; the three after it belong to one bundle each. Name any entry
freely and say which bundle it belongs to.

## Shared daily practice

- **You do not know which skill fits** → lq-start *(all three bundles)*. This
  map, and one pick with the sentence to type. It routes; it never does the
  work, and it is not itself a destination.
- **Completed work needs explaining to a client, a board or a colleague** →
  legaldesign *(litigation and transactional)*. One polished, editable,
  evidence-grounded page or slide brief, built from work already done, that
  opens in the preview pane. It lays out content that already exists; it does
  not write the substance. Not a formatted report or a deck: for those, the
  built-in Word and PowerPoint skills.
- **A regulation or piece of guidance has to be read and applied** → regulatory
  *(litigation and transactional)*. Names the instrument and the publisher to
  get it from, waits for you to put that document in the Input folder, reads
  the version statement off it, and answers by quoting the provisions verbatim
  with the source filename beside them. Nothing is quoted that did not come
  from the publisher's own document, so open-ended research belongs with the
  built-in Deep Research skill.
- **Time entries need narratives** → timenarratives *(litigation and
  transactional)*. Drafts candidate narratives for the named lawyer and matter
  from this conversation and the documents you supply, as an unvalidated
  preview. Deal or dispute, same skill. No hours, no rates, no billability
  calls, and not for case chronologies or fact narratives. Private practice
  only; in-house lawyers skip this.
- **You want to keep what you learned** → wiki *(litigation and
  transactional)*. Your personal legal wiki in a folder you own: linked,
  source-grounded notes that record reusable law and method, never matter
  facts. The layer underneath everything. Not a place for client or party
  facts, matter chronologies or work product.

## Litigation bundle

- **A new dispute needs a controlled start** → new-matter *(litigation)*.
  Captures the matter, forum, posture, authority, conflicts status, urgent
  dates and preservation needs before anything is written or moved, and shows
  the record for approval before it is saved. Organising the documents that
  follow is organize-case-docs.
- **The case file needs a working map** → organize-case-docs *(litigation)*.
  Builds the source inventory, chronology with exact locators, proof chart,
  issue and evidence maps and matter briefing without moving originals.
  Creating the matter record first is new-matter.
- **In U.S. federal civil practice, you are planning preservation, serving
  requests, answering them, or handling a subpoena** → document-discovery
  *(litigation)*. Builds a proportionate preservation package, or drafts
  targeted requests, itemised responses and objections under Rules 34, 33 and
  36, meet-and-confer agendas, privilege-log queues and subpoena triage. Not
  for reviewing a production you have received.
- **A production has arrived and has to be worked document by document** →
  docreview *(litigation)*. Works the incoming production a tranche at a time
  against the matter's requests, holds every privilege candidate until you
  rule, and fills an Excel master register whose first column is the privilege
  state, with the finding, the words it rests on and where they sit. Your own
  side's holds and privilege log are document-discovery.
- **A deposition needs preparing or reconciling** → depositions *(litigation)*.
  Turns the proof plan into witness, topic, exhibit, admission and impeachment
  work, including Rule 30(b)(6), nonparty and transcript follow-through.
- **A brief, regulator paper or client memorandum needs drafting** → writing
  *(litigation)*. Chooses advocacy or neutral analysis, then writes from the
  record with the right candour, authority and review controls. Checking the
  citations afterwards is cite-check.
- **A position or the other side's case needs to survive contact** →
  pressuretest *(litigation)*. Maps the argument, confirms the map with you,
  attacks it every way the material allows, and reports where it breaks —
  logic, dates, figures and cross-document consistency only. Only when you
  expressly ask for it to be pressure-tested. Citations stay with cite-check;
  legal research does not belong here.
- **A filing cites authorities you have in hand** → cite-check *(litigation)*.
  Verifies every citation and quotation against the authorities you supply,
  and catches fabricated case law before filing. It never guesses at a source
  it was not given, and it does not determine whether an authority is still
  good law.
- **A litigation letter or settlement exchange needs drafting** →
  correspondence *(litigation)*. Handles discovery, substantive and settlement
  correspondence with source, formation and no-send review gates. Sending is
  yours; the built-in Email skill does that.
- **The client, co-counsel or portfolio owner needs the status** →
  client-update *(litigation)*. Separates verified developments, analysis,
  recommendations and decisions in matter, event, outside-counsel and
  portfolio formats. It drafts; it never sends.

## Transactional bundle

- **A markup has come back and you need to know what actually moved** →
  read-redline *(transactional)*. Reads a compare document or a file carrying
  tracked changes, rates every change High, Medium or Low for materiality,
  groups them into a handful of themes that say what the other side is driving
  at, and shows the marked passages side by side in the preview pane. It reads
  a document that already carries change marks; it does not compare two clean
  documents.
- **You need to turn approved precedents into reusable positions** →
  playbook-builder *(transactional)*. Builds a source-linked contract playbook
  in Markdown with preferred positions, fallbacks, red lines and
  lawyer-curated Matter Lenses, or imports the playbook table your team
  already keeps. It does not approve inferred policy for you, and it does not
  review live paper.
- **A live draft needs checking against that approved playbook** →
  playbook-review *(transactional)*. Starts from Standard Baseline, applies
  only the Matter Lenses you actively choose, and returns a clause-anchored
  issues list with suggested inline markup and a stated coverage count. It
  accepts substantially equivalent drafting. It does not produce a
  tracked-changes redline.
- **The defined terms in an agreement have to hold together** →
  definition-check *(transactional)*. Reports terms used but never defined,
  defined but never used, defined twice with different meanings, used before
  they are defined, spelled inconsistently, or pointing at a schedule that is
  not there — with an Excel register of every clause read and every term
  found, so the count can be checked.
- **A clause from a precedent has to be refitted to this agreement** → conform
  *(transactional)*. Adapts borrowed drafting into the vocabulary of the
  document being drafted, or finds precedent language nobody adapted. Reads
  both documents' defined terms in the same session and returns a mapping
  table saying which source concept each destination term covers and which
  ones shift the meaning.
- **The deal needs a working closing checklist, or the one you have needs
  updating** → closing-checklist *(transactional)*. Drafts an editable
  checklist from the share purchase agreement or other anchor agreement, or
  proposes and applies changes to your existing one after revised agreements
  or new instructions, keeping house formatting, references and timing. It
  tracks deliverables and conditions to closing: it does not review a draft
  against negotiation positions, chase status, build signature packs or audit
  the closing folder.
- **The set is signed and has to be indexed** → closing-bible
  *(transactional)*. Reads the signed closing folder and hands back an index
  you confirm, an exceptions list, and an Excel status register with one row
  per expected item, the version selected as final and the qualification on
  anything not ready. It is a reading of the documents supplied, not a
  balanced audit, and it never says a closing set is whole.
- **Signature pages have to be prepared and chased** → sigpack
  *(transactional)*. Works out who signs what from the execution versions,
  shows the signing matrix for approval, drafts the pages through the built-in
  Word skill, writes the per-party instructions and the cover note with the
  escrow wording, and keeps the chase list in a ledger you bring back each
  session. It never assembles an executed set and never says a returned page
  is signed.
- **A data room has to be reviewed against an issues list** → diligence
  *(transactional)*. Inventories the room, maps contract families, compiles
  your checklist into a framework you confirm on a sample, then works
  agreement by agreement filling an Excel master register — one row per issue
  per agreement, with the finding, the words it rests on and where they sit —
  and delivers a crosswalk showing what was covered and what was parked.

## Companion bundle

- **You are coming back to this and want the one next move** → legalquants
  *(companion bundle)*. Picks up your own progress with AI and offers the
  single next move that makes sense now, worked out from what you say in this
  conversation and from a note or preferences file you bring. Never a menu,
  never a test, and never the legal work itself; it keeps nothing between
  conversations and says so.
- **A question about how other lawyers work with AI** → lq-ask *(companion
  bundle)*. Answers from a dated library of published work that ships inside
  the skill, cited by title and date rather than by link, and says plainly
  when the library has nothing rather than producing an answer anyway. Outside
  material and anything newer than the library belongs with the built-in Deep
  Research skill.
- **You want to know where you stand with AI** → lq-mirror *(companion
  bundle)*. Twelve short questions and an honest reading — your archetype,
  said in a way that respects you — plus one concrete move for the week.
  Recognition, never a verdict, never a score, and never the legal work
  itself.
- **One piece of work you have just done needs going back over** → lq-reflect
  *(companion bundle)*. Takes this conversation and the files you attach and
  gives back what helped, what got in the way, one change to keep with a
  countable signature, and a short dated note you carry to the next review. It
  reviews the work, not you.
- **You think a session was genuinely the good kind** → my-lq-moment
  *(companion bundle)*. Judges one piece of work in this session against a
  fixed bar and, only when it clears it, writes up what you did, what it took
  and what it changed, with a post in your own voice and a cover card for the
  preview pane. Borderline means not earned.
- **What you have done with AI has to be written up** → lq-apply *(companion
  bundle)*. Turns your own record into a one-page legal-AI CV or a
  professional profile draft, showing an inventory of what it read before it
  drafts anything, keeping what you said apart from what the evidence shows,
  and listing the gaps in plain words instead of filling them. It sends
  nothing anywhere.
- **Somebody in the firm has done this before and you want their name** →
  lq-connect *(companion bundle)*. Writes your need back as a short sanitised
  summary for you to approve, searches the organisation through the built-in
  Enterprise Search skill, and hands over two or three colleagues with the
  document that shows the fit and one line on what to say. It hands over names
  and stops: contacting or booking anyone is yours.
