## Workflow

1. **Inspect the file.** A `.docx` takes the Word path below; the PDF steps 2, 4 and 5 do not apply to it. For a PDF, read it through first and note the page count and, where the last page is a compare-tool summary (Litera and others print totals for insertions, deletions, moves and table changes), the totals printed on it — keep those numbers for step 5. Say which pages you are treating as the body, so a cover page or a summary page is not read as body text.

2. **Calibrate before extracting. Always.**
   Before you read a single change, tell the lawyer in plain words what you can actually see in this file — strikethrough, underlining, coloured text, margin balloons, moved-text markers — and what you are taking each of them to mean. Show it as a short colour-to-role table and ask them to confirm it matches what they see on screen. A wrong role silently inverts every change. Three things to look for:
   - One colour carrying both strikethrough and underlining. Say so, then read the role below word level from the decoration rather than the colour, splitting where deleted and inserted text touch.
   - A colour showing **both** strikes and underlines while the deleted and the inserted colours each show one is usually *moved* text. Ask; if the lawyer confirms, report that passage as a move, not a change at both ends.
   - Text in a colour that is never struck or underlined anywhere: coloured headings, a house style, a cover note. Colour alone is never a change, so it is not counted. Say in one line what it was and that it was left out, and treat it as a change only if the lawyer says their compare tool marks whole insertions or deletions by colour alone.

   **Record the gate.** Write the colour-to-role table, the lawyer's confirmation in their own words and the date into `redline-calibration.md` in the session's Output folder, and reproduce those lines at the head of every deliverable. Nothing here enforces the gate; it is a rule you apply to yourself, and a run nobody confirmed produces a report that looks exactly like one they did. That is why the confirmation travels on the artifact. If they did not confirm — "just go ahead", or the question went unanswered — write that on the artifact in those words, never "confirmed".

3. **Extract the changed pairs.** Work through the document and record every change as a pair of old text and new text with an identifier and a location. A paragraph you cannot reconstruct reliably goes on a quarantine list instead of into the pairs, and you tell the lawyer which pages those are on. Never present the extraction as finished while the quarantine list has anything on it. Moved passages — from the calibration above, or from a compare tool's "Moved from" and "Moved to" labels — are listed separately as moves, not as changes. Save the pairs, the moves and the quarantine list as `extract.json` in the Output folder.

4. **Read every changed page and look at what is on it.** PDF path only. Go page by page over every page carrying a changed pair, plus a sample of the rest; read every page when the calibration was mixed, when anything was quarantined, or when the extraction found no changes at all. Compare what the page shows against that page's pairs. Every visible mark — strikethrough, underline, coloured text that is struck or underlined, a margin balloon, a moved-text marker — with no matching pair becomes a manual-review item appended to the quarantine list in `extract.json`, with the page, what the mark shows in your own words, and where it sits. Never explain a mark away. Coloured text with no strike and no underline is not a mark; it was reported at calibration and is not a change unless the lawyer said so.

   If you cannot tell what is on a page — a scan, an image, a page whose layout you cannot resolve — say so, name the page, and say plainly that the page-by-page reconciliation did not run for it. Do not describe a page you could not read.

5. **Reconcile the counts.** State the number of changed pairs beside the summary-page totals from step 1, where there were any, and explain the gap in one sentence: one pair can cover several summary changes, and a table change may be one summary line and no pair. Both numbers go in the delivery in step 9, not just the conclusion drawn from them — you counted them yourself, so the lawyer should see what you counted. A large unexplained gap means go back to step 4 and read the pages again.

6. **Rate and write the rows.** Group pairs that edit one provision into one row. For each row write `{provision, source_pair_ids, comment, materiality}`: a short label, the pair identifiers it covers, a one-to-two sentence comment on what changed and why it matters in the rubric's voice, and High, Medium or Low per the rubric. Add an optional `direction` — `favours-us`, `favours-them`, `neutral` or `unknown` — per the rubric's direction heuristic; it is orthogonal to the tier and omitted rows render exactly as before. When unsure, a Low row rather than no row. Save as `rows.json` in the Output folder (a JSON list). The keys `provision` and `materiality` are what the skill calls the change's label and significance; use them exactly.

7. **Group into themes.** Rows answer "what changed in this clause"; the reader's question is "what is the other side doing." Two passes over `rows.json` (never re-analyse; rows stay the grounded unit):
   - *Housekeeping bucket.* A row is housekeeping if its change is ONLY renumbering, cross-reference updates caused by renumbering, table-of-contents or pagination shifts, draft-date mechanics, or defined-term tidy-ups with no change in legal effect. When in doubt, not housekeeping.
   - *Themes.* Cluster the rest into 5–15 themes. Each theme: a 2–6 word label and a 1–2 sentence summary that states DIRECTION — what the party proposing the changes is doing ("tightening the merger covenant"), never a generic label ("various definition changes"). Merge across provisions when the same intent appears in scattered clauses; that merging is the point. A row may appear in up to two themes. No single-row themes unless the row is genuinely standalone; more than two of those means under-merging. Rows that fit no theme go in `unclustered` with a one-line reason — never force a fit.
   Save as `themes.json` in the Output folder:
   ```json
   {"themes": [{"label": "", "summary": "", "row_refs": [0]}],
    "housekeeping": {"count": 0, "row_refs": []},
    "unclustered": [{"row_ref": 0, "why": ""}],
    "coverage": {"rows_total": 0, "rows_themed": 0, "rows_housekeeping": 0, "rows_unclustered": 0}}
   ```
   `row_refs` are 0-based indexes into `rows.json`. The count must balance: every row appears exactly once across themes (counting a multi-theme row once), housekeeping and unclustered, and the three counts sum to `rows_total`. Count before you write, and print the four numbers in the delivery so the lawyer can add them up. You did this arithmetic yourself, so it is a statement, not a guarantee.

8. **Build the marked-passage view.** In place of handing back the lawyer's own file with marks on it, write a single self-contained HTML page — `<name> - Marked Passages.html` in the Output folder — that lists each row in document order with its old text beside its new text, its theme, its materiality tier and its direction where the row carries one, and a separate block at the end for the quarantine and manual-review items. Use plain HTML and inline styling only; load nothing from anywhere else. It opens in the preview pane. Tell the lawyer in one line that this is a view of the changes, not their document marked up: the file they sent comes back untouched.

9. **Present.** Lead with the themes: label plus one-line direction each, then the housekeeping line ("N housekeeping provisions — renumbering and pagination, nothing to negotiate"), then any standalone rows. Then the reconciliation line, in numbers: pages read and pages you could not read, marks seen, marks matched to pairs, manual-review items, the summary-page total where there was one, and the theme counts from step 7. Then name every file you left in the Output folder — `extract.json`, `rows.json`, `themes.json`, `redline-calibration.md` and the marked-passage page — and say that they stay there; nothing here can remove them for the lawyer.

   When the lawyer wants a take-away, produce the issues list through the Word skill: a table grouped by theme, one row per provision carrying the tier (with the row's direction beside it when present), the redline itself with deletions and insertions shown as text, the comment, and the row's first location — page for a PDF run, paragraph for a Word run. Put the calibration lines from step 2 at the head and the counts from steps 5 and 7 at the foot. It is the briefing document, deliberately summary-level; the marked-passage page stays the fuller view. The lawyer's own file is never modified.
