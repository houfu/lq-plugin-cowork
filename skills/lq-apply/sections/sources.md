## 1. Sources — lightest first, always their choice

Say in one line, before anything else, that this is not connected to any
application, assessment, profile or directory: it drafts something they own
and keep, and it sends nothing anywhere.

Then establish what to draw on, in this order, stopping where they say:

1. **A record they bring.** Anything they have kept about their own work
   with AI: a dated note from a review, a list of what they have built, an
   existing CV, a write-up of a project. Ask them to attach the files one by
   one — ask for the files, not for a folder. If they say it is already
   sitting in their Cowork folder, ask what it is called, look for it, and
   say in one line whether you found it; never carry on as though you had.
   Where they have an `lqplaybook.md`, read only the confirmed `[lq-apply]`
   entries in it, and never write to it.
   Usable: the practice sentence; how their fluency has changed and when;
   the moments they kept as worth keeping; the frictions they worked through
   (one they dug their way out of is proof of growth); how regularly they
   review their own practice. Never used: raw session logs, unconfirmed or
   proposed items, anything that looks like a client, a matter, or document
   content. No archetype, anywhere — that label is never stored and never
   ships; the draft is evidence, not a diagnosis.
2. **Project material they attach** — an index, a README, a CV, a case
   study. Read the index first; project summaries are enough to represent
   the main areas. A large set of files is not permission to read client
   matters or every personal file; the scope is their choice.
3. **A public handle they name** — a code host, a portfolio, a newsletter.
   Take it as reported and label it that way everywhere it is used. Nothing
   here opens it, so never describe what is on it and never attribute work
   to them on the strength of a name.
4. **A short conversation** about their experience, when nothing else
   exists.

**Nothing kept, nothing attached:** say so honestly and compile from what
they give you in this session. That is a normal way to run this, not a
degraded one — never send them away to come back with a file.

Name every file you actually read before you use any of it. A draft built on
a record that was never found reads exactly like a full one, which is why the
inventory in §2 comes before the highlights.

This skill writes nothing but the draft and its evidence notes. If something
in the session is worth keeping for next time, offer it as one exact line
they can add to their own file.
