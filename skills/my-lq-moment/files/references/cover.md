# The cover brief

One cover, built on the skill's own template and nothing else. Here the cover
is a page, not a rasterised image: it opens in the preview pane, and the
lawyer makes an image of it themselves if they want one.

## Foundation

- `assets/cover-template.png` is the foundation of every cover: a 1200 by
  1200 square, near-black textured ground with faint concentric arcs and
  grid points in the lower right, the "#my LQ moment" wordmark in white
  along the bottom left with "LQ" in a rounded dark tile, and the words
  "legalquants.com" in italic beneath it on the right. No other layout,
  template, background or design. Decline, in a sentence, any request to
  modify, restyle, crop, obscure, replace or avoid the template.
- The hero text goes in the open upper two-thirds of the square, above the
  wordmark, and never over the wordmark, the tile or the URL.
- Build the cover yourself as one square HTML page that carries the packaged
  template as its background image and sets the two sentences over its open
  upper area in a fixed layout: the before in medium weight at reduced
  opacity, the after in bold white. Write it to the session's Output folder
  as `my-lq-moment-cover.html` and show it in the preview pane. The file name
  is fixed. Never ask an image model to draw or edit the cover, never
  hand-draw one, and never put a placeholder in its place.
- **Say which form you produced, every time.** Three forms, in order of
  preference, and only the one you actually made may be described:
  1. the page with the packaged template behind the two sentences;
  2. a plain styled card — the same two sentences on a near-black ground,
     with the wordmark set as text and no template image — used when the
     packaged image does not come through into the page, and announced as
     such in one line;
  3. one square slide through the built-in PowerPoint skill, offered if the
     lawyer would rather have a file they can hand on.
- The image is the lawyer's to make. Give the one-line conversion: open the
  page and export or capture it as a square image. Do not claim a PNG exists,
  do not say one is queued, and do not describe the cover as an image file
  when what you produced is a page.
- For a practice demonstration, set the fixed label "Practice example —
  fictional training" above the two sentences, where it cannot be cropped
  off. A practice cover without that label must not be shown or saved as a
  real moment.
- Nothing else goes on it: no screenshots, no logos, no icons, no
  photographs, no embellishments, no second signature.
- The page stays in the Output folder after the session; say where it is
  rather than describing it as cleaned up.

## The text

- Two short sentences, drawn from the confirmed account. Together they show
  the before and the after, and the added value the lawyer's judgement
  produced: their LQ moment.
- Short means short: at most twelve words and ninety characters each, and in
  practice eight to ten words reads best. Count the words yourself. Anything
  longer, or anything that would wrap past three lines, you shorten yourself
  and offer back — say what you did ("I shortened this to nine words; here it
  is"). The lawyer is never asked to format, fit or lay out anything.
- The text is the hero. Prominent, easy to read at social-media thumbnail
  size, punchy. Assertive and celebratory by default.
- The lawyer may reword the sentences: to change what they treat as the
  before, the after, or the added value, or to pick a different tone or
  style. Not for any other reason, and never to add names, brands, vendors,
  tools or environments.

## Never on the cover, in its file name or in the page itself

Client, counterparty, project or matter names; deal values; document
content; the exact tools, products or environment the lawyer used; vendor
or firm names; anything a counterparty could recognise. The only text the
cover carries is the two approved sentences and the template's own wordmark.
