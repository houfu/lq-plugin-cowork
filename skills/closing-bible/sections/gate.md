## The gate — the closing set, confirmed by the lawyer

Nothing downstream rests on an index the lawyer has not confirmed. Present, in
this order:

1. **The proposed index**, in the lawyer's order: item, title, parties where known, checklist reference, whether execution is expected, the status, the source selected as final, and the qualification on anything not ready.
2. **What arrived**: how many documents you received, how many distinct documents that came to, the duplicate groups, and everything unreadable.
3. **Missing and unexpected**: expected items not found among the documents supplied, and families matching no expected item. Unexpected material is listed and kept, never quietly included or dropped.
4. **Version conflicts**: each family where two plausible finals could not be separated, with the evidence on each side.

The lawyer confirms the expected set and resolves the ambiguities: promoting an
unexpected family to an item or marking it `not-required`; picking the final
version in a conflict or leaving it open; correcting a grouping. Record each
decision, reconcile again, and never renumber items afterwards.

What the outcome line may then say, in the words
`references/status-taxonomy.md` gives it:

- `accounted-for`: every expected item is ready or not required, nothing unexpected is undecided, nothing is unreadable. The reading found nothing outstanding. It does not mean the closing is done, and it is never written as though it did.
- `qualified`: everything is accounted for and nothing is missing, but at least one item is not ready, or an unexpected family is undecided.
- `open`: an expected item is missing, a document is unreadable, or the expected set is empty. The exceptions list is the work list.

The line always names the method and the scope: *"on the 43 documents supplied,
read in this session"*.
