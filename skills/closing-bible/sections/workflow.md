## Workflow

1. **Take stock of what arrived.** Say the count first — *"I received 43 documents"*. Then list each: filename, apparent title, the parties it names, its date if it carries one, and whether it looks executed or a draft. Identical copies under different names are noted as such and both kept. Anything that could not be opened or read is listed as unreadable with the reason and stays visible in every later step. Nothing is decided here, and there are no fingerprints: this skill cannot say a document is unchanged since it looked at it, and says so once rather than implying otherwise.

2. **Group the documents into families.** A family is one document across its versions and duplicates. Group on what the documents say — title, parties, defined terms, execution pages — and on filenames only as a supporting signal, never the deciding one. Where the lawyer has a checklist, group against its rows; otherwise propose an index from the families.

3. **Show the family table and confirm the grouping.** One row per family: apparent title, the files in it, what grouped them, the checklist row it matched. Ask the lawyer to split families holding two different documents and merge families that are one document under two names. Every distinct file sits in exactly one family, and nothing goes on until the grouping is confirmed.

4. **Look at each family and record what you saw.** This is the attention bill. For every family, with the whole family in view:

   - **The pick** — which member is final, with the evidence separating it from the others: a version marker, matching execution pages, completeness. Where the evidence does not separate two plausible finals, record `version-conflict`, pick nothing, and stop that family there.
   - **Execution** — where it is expected, read the execution pages of the pick and record `appears-signed`, `appears-incomplete`, `appears-unsigned` or `unclear`; whether it is dated and what date is printed, verbatim; and where the signature pages sit. Where execution is not expected, both are `not-expected`.
   - **A page you cannot read is not a page you read.** Where the execution pages are a scan or an image, record `not-inspected` with the reason, propose `unsigned`, and let the reconciliation carry it as not ready. Never guess a status from a filename, and never describe a page you did not read.
   - **Missing components** — every schedule, annex, exhibit or page the document refers to and does not attach, and where the reference was seen.
   - **The evidence** — one line per observation: the claim, the document, where you saw it, what you saw. Write what you saw, never a conclusion; an observation about another member of the family is not evidence about the pick.
   - **The status**, derived from those findings by the table in `references/status-taxonomy.md` and never the other way round.

   Work through the families one at a time. A family nobody looked at is named under "Not inspected", not quietly carried.

5. **Reconcile, and build the register.** Match the families to the expected set — the lawyer's checklist first, else the index drafted from the families — and give every item one status. Then write the register described in `references/status-register.md`, and state two numbers in the reply: the rows read from any register they attached, and the rows written.

   The register is the substitute for the balance the original computed, and not the same thing. Its honesty comes from being inspectable: 43 rows with a status column can be checked in ten seconds, and a sentence saying everything is accounted for cannot be checked at all.

6. **Present at the gate below**, then the outcome line. Where the lawyer resolves something there, record it, reconcile again, and show the line again.
