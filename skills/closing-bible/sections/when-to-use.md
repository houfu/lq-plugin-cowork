## When to use

- Read a closing folder after signing and propose an index: what each document is, which version is final and why, whether it appears executed and dated, whether the schedules and annexes it refers to are attached.
- Reconcile that against the agreed closing checklist, or draft an index from the documents themselves where there is no checklist, for the lawyer to confirm.
- Write the exceptions list: what the checklist expects and the documents do not contain, what is duplicated, what is in version conflict, what could not be read.
- Hand it back as a status register the lawyer can sort and count themselves.

Not for:

- Deciding that legal completion has occurred, or saying the set is whole. It cannot.
- Vouching for due execution, signatory authority, delivery, dating authority or enforceability.
- Assembling a combined bible PDF, merging or splitting documents, or binding anything.
- Replacing missing signature pages, or executing or dating documents.
- Treating a filename or a file date as an execution date.
- Sending the bible, filing it into a document management system or changing a contract register.
- Renaming, moving, overwriting or deleting a source document.
- Due diligence or playbook review of the documents.
