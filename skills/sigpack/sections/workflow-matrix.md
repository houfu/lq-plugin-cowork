## Workflow — the signing matrix and the pages

1. **Read the signing matrix out of the execution versions.** Take each document the lawyer attaches, read its parties clause and its execution language, and propose, per document and per party: the capacity chain, the signatory where the document names one, the required marks (a deed needs a witness; a company signing by two directors needs both), and how many originals that party must sign. `references/signature_page_rules.md` is the authority on all three of party, signatory and capacity, and on what is not a signature page — an exhibit's form execution page looks perfect and nobody signs it.

   Blank fields are `Unknown` and the row is flagged, never guessed. Say how many documents you read and name any you could not.

2. **Gate: show the lawyer the matrix table and confirm before drafting.** Document, page, party, signatory, capacity, copies, required marks. This is the step that catches a misread execution block, and it is the only thing standing between a misreading and a set of pages that go out wrong — so it is not optional and it is not summarised. Wait for the confirmation.

3. **Draft the pages on the firm's own template.** Ask the lawyer to attach their signature-page template as a Word document, with the placeholders it uses for the document title, the party, the signatory and the title. One page per party per document, produced through the built-in Word skill, with the footer the template carries. Show one drafted page and get it approved before the rest go out.

   Drafting happens before circulation and is the lawyer's to approve. Nothing is ever added to a page after the parties have agreed the version or after it has been signed — no stamps, tags, watermarks or annotations. The executed document has to be page-identical to the version the parties agreed.

4. **Open the ledger, or update the one you were given.** One row per signature page per block, from the confirmed matrix: the document, the page, the party, the signatory, the capacity, the copies required, and the status. Every block starts `required`. `references/ledger_schema.md` gives the columns. If the lawyer attached a ledger for this matter, add to it rather than starting a new one, and say how many rows you read and how many you wrote.
