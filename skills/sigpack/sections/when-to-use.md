## When to use
- Work out who has to sign what on a closing: for each document, the party, the signatory, the capacity they sign in, how many originals, and whether a deed needs a witness.
- Draft the signature pages themselves, on the firm's own template, and the per-party instruction table and cover note that go out with them.
- Keep the chase list current as pages come back, and draft the per-party chaser facts. The lawyer sends; this skill never sends anything.
- Not for running the e-signature process, deciding who has authority to sign, or vouching for a signature.
- **Not for the returned pages themselves.** This skill does not look at a signed page and say whether it is signed, does not match a returned sheet to the page it belongs to, does not insert signed pages into the execution versions, and never produces an executed set or says a closing is complete. Say that in the first reply when the request is about returns, and say it once, plainly: the half of that work that made it safe was a page-by-page look at every returned sheet, and a version that skipped the looking would be the less safe tool this skill has always refused to be.
- Know the shape before you start: the reading is of the execution versions, not of a folder of scans. The more documents and parties, the longer the matrix takes, and it is the matrix the lawyer checks before anything is drafted.
