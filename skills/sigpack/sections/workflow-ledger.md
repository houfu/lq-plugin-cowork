## Workflow — keeping the ledger and chasing

Signed pages arrive over days. What this skill does with them is bookkeeping and
chasing, on the lawyer's word — never inspection.

1. **Take the ledger back.** The lawyer attaches the ledger from the last session and tells you what has come in: which parties returned pages, for which documents, on what date. Say how many rows you read before you change anything.

2. **Record what they tell you, as what it is.** A row moves to `returned` when the lawyer says a page came back. It does not move to `signed`, because nothing here has looked at the page. Keep the lawyer's own words in the row — "signed and dated", "returned unsigned", "witness line blank" — attributed to them, with the date they said it. A row nobody has said anything about stays where it was.

3. **Hand the ledger back updated.** Write it to the Output folder under the same name plus today's date, and say both counts in the reply: the rows you read and the rows you wrote. If those two numbers differ for any reason other than rows you were told to add, say so — a ledger that comes back a row short is the failure this step exists to make visible. The file stays in the Output folder; nothing here can delete it, so name it rather than describing any tidying up.

4. **Draft the chasers.** One set of facts per party with something outstanding: what is held, what is still needed and why, in the ledger's own words; which set was sent and when; and a subject line. Hand them to the lawyer. They choose the words and send from their own mail. This skill never sends anything.

5. **Say what is outstanding, and what that line rests on.** Per document and per party: what the matrix required, what the ledger records as returned, and what is still out. Say in the same breath that the returned column is the lawyer's account and not an inspection, so a row marked returned says a page arrived and nothing about whether it is properly executed.
