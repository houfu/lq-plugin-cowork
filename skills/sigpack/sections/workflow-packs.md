## Workflow — the sets, the instructions and the cover note

1. **Settle the build options** in this order — what the lawyer said in the request, then their `[sigpack]` playbook defaults, then ask for whatever is still open: grouping (by agreement, by counterparty or by signatory), copies (one by default, or per block from the matrix), whether a page shared by two parties goes into each party's set (yes by default), and the filename pattern (`Signature Pack – [Group]` by default; follow theirs exactly, including what to leave out).

2. **Produce one set per group.** A set is the drafted pages for that group, in document then page order, each a separate document named to the pattern. Not a merged PDF: this skill does not assemble pages into one file. Where a set is large, the lawyer can download the session's outputs together as an archive — up to 50 files and 500 MB — and that ceiling is worth naming when a closing is big enough to reach it.

   Mark the blocks `sent` on the ledger and record what went to whom and when.

3. **Write the instruction table.** One row per party: party, document, sign as (capacity), by (signatory), copies, and any required witness. It is the table the recipient works from, so it names documents the way the lawyer named them, not the way the files are named.

4. **Write the cover note**, from the playbook or from the default: the pages attached and the copies to sign; return by [date]; signed pages held in escrow, undated, and released only on the lawyer's instruction once the documents are in execution form; the lawyer will be asked before release. The escrow line ships by default; a firm overrides it in the playbook.

5. **Present.** The headline (N sets by [grouping], M pages, K rows flagged), the instruction table, the cover note, and the pages. Then the standing line, in these words or words like them: *drafted N pages across M documents for P parties; sent none / sent on [date]; outstanding Q.* Name the method — that count is the ledger's rows, read back — and do not dress it as a balance anybody struck: the set is never described as complete, and no page is ever described as signed. Nothing here has looked at a signed page.
