# The matching taxonomy (verified 2026-09-05)

## Practice areas (the seven)

Contract Review & Drafting · Compliance & Regulatory · Litigation &
Disputes · Practice Infrastructure & Tooling · Legal Research · Corporate &
Transactional · IP & Patents

Map the user's words to the nearest area; when their need spans two, pick
the one the *question* is about, not the one their practice is in.

## Jurisdiction

However it is written down ("New York; England & Wales") — the first
question is qualified-where, not sitting-where. Use when the need is
jurisdiction-shaped; ignore otherwise.

## Stack (what they build with)

The tools someone is on the record using. Use when the need is tool-shaped
("someone who ships document viewers", "has wired up a contract pipeline").
Match what is written literally; don't infer equivalents.

## Known-for and visible work

A person's own "mainly does…" line, wherever it appears, is the best topic
signal you have. Work you can see — a note, a write-up, a matter record, a
listed project with its area and description — is stronger: prefer someone
whose visible work *is* the thing the user is trying to do over someone who
merely shares a practice area.

## Outside writing and repositories — evidence, not a matching signal

A personal site, a newsletter, a repository or a professional network entry
never decides *who* matches; the taxonomy above does that. Once someone is
picked, such a thing is named as what the source said about it, never
described as though it had been read. A thing you have not opened is
mentioned, not quoted (see `SKILL.md` §2).

## What's deliberately not used

Seniority and job title, which say what someone is allowed to sign rather
than what they have done; anything a person has not themselves put on the
record; and anything this session could not open. Visible means visible to
this lawyer in this session, full stop.
