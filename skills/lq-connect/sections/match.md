## 2. Match against the people this session can actually see

Before the search starts, say so in one line: finding a good match may
take a minute or two. While the search runs, give brief progress beats
("still looking — widening to adjacent practice areas"); a long search is
never silent.

There are two pools. Say which one each name came from.

- **Their own organisation.** Hand the approved summary to the Enterprise
  Search skill and ask it for the work first and the people second: the
  notes, write-ups, memos and matter material the organisation holds on this
  kind of problem, and who is named in them. A person is a match because
  something they actually worked on is the thing the lawyer is about to do.
- **A list the lawyer brings.** If they attach or paste a directory export,
  a membership list, a conference attendee list or their own contacts,
  match against that too. A brought list is only as current as the day it
  was exported; say so once, and never treat it as the whole field.

Match on the taxonomy in `references/taxonomy.md`:

- **Practice area** (the seven-area taxonomy), **jurisdiction** when the
  question is qualified-where, **stack** entries when the need is
  tool-shaped, **known-for** signals and visible work for topic fit.
- **Only what you actually read.** Someone the search does not surface does
  not exist for this skill — never mention them, never hint that they might
  be out there. A document this session cannot open is not evidence either:
  name it as unopened and move on.
- Pick **two or three** — fewer when that is what the evidence supports,
  never pad — and never a list of ten. Two well-grounded names beat three,
  and one beats two padded out. If the evidence supports nobody, that is the
  answer.
- **Every why carries its source.** For each person: the name, one line on
  why they fit *this* need, and the document, note or list entry that put
  them there, named and quoted. A citation is something you read and can
  quote, or it is not a citation.
- **Mentioned is not read.** Where what you read points at someone's own
  writing, a repository or an outside page, name it as mentioned there. Never
  describe a page you have not read.

Nobody fits well? Say so plainly, say what you searched and what you could
not see, and suggest the search the lawyer could run themselves — the terms
that would have surfaced the right person if the organisation had one. A
forced match is worse than an honest miss.
