## Intake

Ask one question at a time. Skip any the user has already answered — most people
open with question 1 unprompted. Never ask all of these at once.

**Before you ask for the document, ask only these two:**

1. What's the problem? Tell me the way you'd tell a colleague.
2. Is there a specific law or rule in play, or are you trying to work out what
   applies?

**Then get the text.** If they have already attached the publisher's document,
use it immediately. Otherwise name the instrument and the publisher and ask them
to download it into the Input folder, as step 3 sets out. Establish the
jurisdiction and the research or as-of date before settling on a version; ask
only if missing and material. Ask about activity, role, location and missing
facts only when they are needed to answer the question. Do not demand a client
name. A material version problem merits a short progress update; it does not
replace the answer-first delivery.

`check` runs this order differently, because there is no instrument to ask for
until the conduct is described. Its section says how.

Do **not** ask about size or thresholds — the instrument generates those, and
asking first anchors the analysis. Do not ask "which regulators do you watch";
that is a monitoring product's question, and this skill starts before you know
what catches you. Do not ask about sector or output format.
