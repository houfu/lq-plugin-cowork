# Deciding what a candidate is

A checklist for step 2. Take the candidates one at a time and give each one a decision from this list. Decide from the source context in front of you, never from the spelling alone, and never from how often the expression appears.

## The seven decisions

- **Defined** — the document assigns this label a meaning: "means", "refers to", collective wording, a quoted parenthetical short form, or equivalent language. Quote the assigned meaning exactly as the document has it, including its qualifications and exclusions, and excluding the label, the quotation marks and the linking words.
- **Alias** — the document expressly ties this label and a confirmed defined label to the same antecedent. Similar spelling is not an alias. An alias must point at a label you have confirmed as defined; if the anchor does not hold up, look again for a direct definition before rejecting the label.
- **Undefined** — a reusable contractual label with no meaning assigned anywhere in this document, even where it is used once and inside another definition. Independent reuse is not required. A clause importing definitions from another agreement does not close a local gap: record the outside document and the supporting wording, and keep the term undefined.
- **External reference** — operative incorporation language identifies a specifically named outside document that was not supplied. Informational. Never report an identified outside document as an undefined term.
- **Not a term** — ordinary or descriptive language, a generic phrase, a fragment, an unquoted explanatory parenthetical, or a section or article title. Judge contractual function: sitting inside another definition, or appearing only once, never justifies rejection on its own.
- **Proper name** — the expression identifies a person, organisation, product, place or other named entity and does nothing else. If the document expressly assigns it as a contractual label, it is defined or an alias instead; contractual function takes precedence over proper-name status.
- **Unresolved** — say which: the identity of the term is ambiguous, the surrounding context is incomplete, or the evidence cannot support a reliable call at all. An unresolved candidate is reported as unresolved; it is never quietly dropped and never displayed as a confirmed defined term.

## Rules that decide the hard cases

Lack of a definition alone does not make ordinary wording undefined. The one exception is unexplained mid-sentence capitalisation: where the same single word appears lowercase elsewhere in the document and capitalised mid-sentence here, treat the capitalised use as a contractual label. Sentence starts, list starts, headings, titles and proper names are ordinary grammatical capitalisation and are excluded.

A parenthetical short form is a definition, not an alias, even when it abbreviates a name. In `Acme, LLC ("Acme")`, `Acme` is **defined**, with `Acme, LLC` as its exact meaning. The thing referred to need not itself be a defined term, and its proper-name status does not invalidate the label.

Reserve **alias** for two or more contractual labels expressly assigned to the same antecedent. In `the supplying entity ("Supplier" or "Vendor")`, `Supplier` is defined, with the meaning `the supplying entity`, and `Vendor` is an alias of `Supplier`. Prefer a substantive label over a pronoun; otherwise take them in the order the document uses.

Where a candidate's meaning is said to sit somewhere you have not read, go and read it before deciding. Prefer operative wording over headings, titles and fragments of proper names.

## What each decision must carry

A decision is worth nothing without the evidence under it. For every candidate record the unit it sits in, the wording you relied on quoted exactly, and — for a defined term — the definition text quoted exactly. Keep the quotation the document's own: never tidy its spelling, spacing or capitalisation, and never paraphrase a definition into what you think it means.
