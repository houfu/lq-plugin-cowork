# Deciding what a use is

A checklist for step 3. For every place an accepted term or alias appears outside its own definition, read the sentence around it and decide what that appearance actually is. Exact spelling and capitalisation are evidence only; they never establish that the defined concept is being invoked.

## The decisions

- **A use of the defined term** — the context invokes the accepted meaning. A singular, plural, possessive, spacing or case variation may still be a use.
- **Ordinary language** — generic or descriptive prose that does not invoke the definition. A term sitting inside a longer document title, a heading or an ordinary compound is frequently this.
- **Part of a name** — the match appears only inside a person, organisation, product, place or other named entity, and does not invoke the definition.
- **Inconsistent capitalisation** — the context invokes the definition but changes the canonical capitalisation without an allowed presentation variant or alias. A singular, plural or possessive change alone is not enough. Ordinary capitalisation of an all-capitals label, all-capitals headings and signature-block typography, and a lowercase alias capitalised at the start of a sentence are all permitted and are not findings.
- **Covered by a longer term** — a longer accepted label containing this one is the term the context actually invokes. Use this only where the longer expression is itself an accepted term, not merely because an ordinary phrase happens to contain the match.
- **Unresolved** — say whether more context might resolve it or the evidence cannot support a call.

## Rules

Apply the longest match. Where one match sits wholly inside a longer accepted label at the same place, the longer one wins and the shorter one is not reported there — while the shorter term's genuinely standalone uses elsewhere stay visible. Where two accepted labels overlap without either containing the other, keep both and look at them together.

Two overlapping terms are both uses only where the context genuinely invokes both.

A single appearance inside another definition can still be a contractual use; do not require independent reuse. Exclude only the introducing label itself, not the later mentions inside its own qualifications and exclusions.

The same spelling can come out differently in different places in the document. Decide each appearance where it sits; do not accept or reject a spelling globally.

Record, for every appearance: its unit, the sentence quoted exactly, and the decision. The count of uses per term in the register is the count of appearances you decided were uses — say so, rather than presenting it as the number of times the term occurs in the file.
