# The sweep for candidates

A checklist for step 2, and again at the end of step 3. The object is recall, not judgment: be over-inclusive here and decide nothing. A candidate is not a finding, and nothing on this list is reported to the lawyer as it stands.

Sweep each unit for:

- Quoted labels in any quotation mark the document uses — straight double quotes, curly quotes, guillemets — including quoted labels inside parentheses and inside collective wording.
- Parenthetical short forms, whether or not they are quoted, and whether or not they abbreviate a name.
- Collective definitions: "together, the ...", "each a ... and together the ...".
- Capitalised expressions used as though they were labels, including multi-word ones, and including the same word capitalised mid-sentence where the document uses it lowercase elsewhere.
- Labels the quoted-label sweep will not catch by shape: unquoted parentheses, single quotation marks, labels running over a line break, labels containing no letters, and labels longer than a line. These are exactly the drafting forms a mechanical sweep misses, so read for them deliberately.
- Expressions that look defined but are not: a term "as defined in the Master Agreement", a term given a meaning by a schedule, a term used with a capital because it opens a list item.

A heading is never a reason to drop a candidate. Note that a candidate came from a heading and let the decision in step 2 deal with it.

Record for each candidate: its unit, the exact spelling as the document has it, and a short note on why it was picked up. Preserve the spelling exactly — never normalise it on the way in, because a variant spelling is itself one of the findings this review exists to produce.

Finish the sweep before deciding anything. If a genuinely new candidate turns up later, add it and re-run the decision step over the additions rather than leaving the list half-decided.
