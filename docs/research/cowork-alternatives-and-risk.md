# Cowork-native alternatives and risk tiers for the fourteen excluded skills

19 September 2026

Fourteen skills were excluded from the Cowork bundles: eight document-pipeline skills (read-redline,
sigpack, closing-bible, definition-check, conform, diligence, docreview, regulatory) and six
companion skills (legalquants, lq-ask, lq-connect, lq-reflect, lq-apply, my-lq-moment). This document
merges two analysts' designs for what Cowork can do instead, reconciles every evidence letter against
a reader's extraction of Microsoft's own pages, enforces one tier scale across both halves, unifies
the probe numbering, and sets out the decision the maintainer has to make.

The maintainer's direction governs the whole document: *"We want to find possible alternatives (for
example save it on OneDrive instead of a store etc.); the buckets might determine the risk we take
that it won't work, not to give up on them."* The tiers below are therefore a risk appetite to choose
against, not a verdict. No skill is recommended out.

### 1.2 Three risk appetites

Baseline bundle occupancy today: **litigation 15 of 20, transactional 8 of 20.**

#### Appetite A — Tier 1 only

**What ships:** `regulatory` alone.

**What the lawyer gets:** they ask what a regulation requires; the skill names the instrument and the
publisher and waits; they drop the official PDF in and confirm in one line where they got it; they
get a note quoting the provision verbatim with its clause, the version and "as at" date, and what it
means for their question, with the source filename and their own provenance line at the bottom. Skip
the download and the note comes back headed "provisional and unverified" with the unproved points
named — which is the skill working, not failing.

**What the tester programme must run first:** nothing. No probe gates it. P6 (web search) and P4
(scanned official PDFs) inform the description and the degrade wording but do not hold the build.

**Bundle arithmetic:** litigation 15 → **16**, transactional 8 → **9**. Four and eleven slots spare.

#### Appetite B — Tiers 1 and 2

**What ships:** regulatory, read-redline, definition-check, diligence, docreview, my-lq-moment.

**What the lawyer gets:** the regulatory note above; a redline read with themes, an issues list and an
old-beside-new page, honest about what it could not see inside the file; a defined-terms review with
the real problems on top and a sortable workbook under it; a data-room review that works a tranche at
a time into a workbook with visible coverage arithmetic and never says "certified"; the same over a
litigation production with a privilege column that stops the run when it is missing; and a private
"was that actually impressive" receipt with a cover card. Six skills, four of which hand back a
workbook the lawyer owns.

**What the tester programme must run first:** **P3** (does Cowork report tracked changes) gates
read-redline and is the single most valuable probe in the set; **P4** (page inspection including
scans) decides read-redline's PDF path and how many documents diligence and docreview park;
**P9** (workbook fidelity on round trip) decides whether the register pattern carries formulas or
only rows; **P11** (what a skill can see of its own session) decides my-lq-moment's evidence step.
P7a is free today against the shipped `legaldesign` templates and should be run first because it
costs nothing.

**Bundle arithmetic:** litigation 15 → **18**, transactional 8 → **13**. Two and seven slots spare.

#### Appetite C — Tiers 1 to 3

**What ships:** all fourteen — the six above plus signature-pages, closing-index, clause-fit,
lq-apply, lq-connect, lq-reflect, lq-ask, and legalquants folded into `lq-start` as a mode.

**What the lawyer gets:** everything in B, plus signature pages drafted on their own template with a
per-party instruction table; a closing index and exceptions list over two hundred documents in a
workbook they sort themselves; a precedent clause refitted to their agreement's vocabulary with the
meaning-changing substitutions escalated; a legal-AI CV in which every line traces to something they
confirmed and the gaps are shown rather than filled; two or three colleagues who have actually done
the thing, each grounded in a document the skill quotes; a review of the piece of work they just
finished with one change to keep; and a practitioner's answer from a dated community library. What
they never get, in any of these, is a sentence claiming completeness that a script used to compute.

**What the tester programme must run first:** everything in B, plus **P14** (Enterprise Search as a
people finder) before lq-connect, and **P11** before lq-reflect — P11 is load-bearing there, not
advisory. P2 (cross-session read-back) upgrades four cards at once and should be run early even
though nothing waits on it.

**Bundle arithmetic:** transactional 8 → **20, exactly at the cap, with no room for anything ever
again**. Litigation 15 → **22, two over the cap of 20**. Appetite C does not fit as designed. The
maintainer must either hold two Tier 3 companion skills out of the litigation bundle, or merge more
of them (legalquants is already folded into `lq-start`; lq-apply into `writing` and lq-connect's
bring-your-own mode into lq-connect are the next candidates), or raise the cap. Note the constraint
that makes splitting expensive: a skill may hand off by name only to skills present in every bundle
it ships in, so a companion that ships transactional-only loses its named hand-offs from the
litigation side and has to fall back to scope statements.

### 1.3 Build order across all fourteen

Ordered by tier, then probe readiness, then surgery size, then shared work.

1. **regulatory (A)** — the only Tier 1 in the set, no gating probe, and the only one of the fourteen
   whose failure mode was written by its own author ("provisional and unverified"). It also proves
   the bring-your-own-source pattern that four other designs lean on.
2. **my-lq-moment** — smallest surgery of the six companions, highest fidelity, no policy block, and
   the repo has already performed this exact operation once in public on `lq-mirror`. Ships now with
   the plain-cover degrade; P7b is an upgrade, not a gate.
3. **definition-check** — Tier 2 with no gating probe and, after reconciliation, **Low** capability
   risk. Fills a gap nothing in either bundle covers. Heavy card, but the heaviness is deletion
   (a deterministic path, an agentic path and a vendor waiver all go), which is the cheapest kind.
4. **read-redline** — Tier 2, gated on **P3**. Third in the queue rather than first because it cannot
   start until P3 has run; but P3 should be the first probe the programme runs, because its result
   also tells you whether P10 is worth running at all.
5. **diligence** — Tier 2. Before docreview, because `references/shared/` is shared byte-for-byte
   between them and the rewrite is done once and used twice.
6. **docreview** — Tier 2, marginal cost small once diligence is done, but it must not ship before a
   litigator has ruled on the self-applied privilege hold (decision 3 below). That decision, not a
   probe, is its gate.
7. **lq-apply** — Tier 3, small card, Low risk, and the gap discipline ("Gaps are asked, never filled")
   is the part a lawyer cannot get from a generic CV drafter. Conditional on the maintainer accepting
   a CV skill with no destination.
8. **signature-pages** (sigpack A) — Tier 3, no probes, small, and `closing-checklist`'s shipped
   description already reserves the ground and only needs its hand-off direction filled in.
9. **clause-fit** (conform) — Tier 3, no probes, small, and "make this precedent clause fit our
   defined terms" is a daily transactional ask whose escalation rubric ports intact.
10. **lq-connect** — Tier 3, cheapest companion surgery (one companion file, untouched), after P14.
11. **lq-reflect** — Tier 3, biggest companion surgery and the sharpest collision with `lq-mirror`,
    after P11. Its one mechanic nothing else in either bundle has is checking next time whether the
    change held.
12. **closing-index** — Tier 3, heaviest re-scope of the eight pipeline skills, and the register
    pattern should be proved by P9 before two hundred closing documents are poured into it.
13. **lq-ask** — Tier 3, and blocked on somebody writing the library rather than on anything Cowork
    can or cannot do. Say that to the maintainer plainly: this is a content commission.
14. **legalquants** — folded into `lq-start` as a returning-lawyer mode, last, because what it should
    say depends on which of the other companions actually shipped.

Deferred behind all fourteen: **sigpack 2B** (needs P4 and P8 and one of P2/P9 — a probe set, not a
probe), **regulatory 8B** and **lq-ask B** (Tier 4, each needs a server somebody operates).

### 1.4 The decisions only the maintainer can make

1. **The external-link carve-outs.** Section 8 of `docs/CONTRACT.md` says "No shipped skill links to
   an external site, sign-up, assessment or product; the only URL a shipped skill may carry is the
   upstream repository in the licence notice." Three separable questions sit under it, and the
   validator does not decide any of them — `LQC-W010` is a **warning**, not an error, and it exempts
   any URL occurring anywhere in the upstream skill folder, so every LegalQuants URL in these six
   skills would pass the build. The block is the policy sentence alone.
   - **(a) Citation by title and date, no URL.** lq-ask can cite "an essay title, a date, a named
     piece of work" without carrying a link. Reading of the rule: permitted, because no link exists.
     Needs confirming, because it is the whole basis of lq-ask's alternative.
   - **(b) Relaying a link the lawyer supplied.** lq-connect's bring-your-own-directory mode relays
     profile links from a file the lawyer attached. `LQC-W010` scans the skill's own `*.md` files and
     section 8 is about what a shipped skill *links to*, not what a lawyer brings into their own
     session. Reading: permitted. It is close enough to the line that it should be an explicit
     decision rather than an inference.
   - **(c) Whether the doors themselves stay shut.** The Residency (legalquants), the Substack and
     `github.com/LegalQuants` (lq-ask), the community directory and profile pages (lq-connect), and
     `assess.legalquants.com` (lq-apply) are all currently removed on policy. For lq-apply this is
     **decisive for the format**: remove the destination and the application format goes with it,
     leaving a CV and a profile. That is a product decision, not a capability one.
2. **The vocabulary rule.** Shipped skills stop using **receipt, certified, proved** — and the same
   applies to **complete** — wherever the arithmetic behind the word is self-performed rather than
   computed by something the lawyer can inspect independently. Six of the eight pipeline skills
   promise a coverage claim a script used to compute; in every case the answer is the same shape:
   make the count visible in an artifact the lawyer can check, state the method that produced it, and
   never use a word that claims more than a careful reading. This is cheap to adopt and expensive to
   retrofit, so it should be decided before build 1, not after build 6. It also has a shipped-tree
   consequence: existing cards should be swept for the same four words.
3. **The docreview privilege hold — a question for a litigator, not for a probe.** Upstream, the hold
   is an enforced record: "Only `not-privileged` releases a unit. Pending, `privileged`, and
   `needs-review` records remain held across every lens" (docreview Q7). In Cowork it becomes a rule
   a model applies to itself with the production in its context. Four mitigations are designed in
   (privilege state as column one; the held count and held ids stated before anything is produced; no
   quoted text from a held unit written anywhere, including into the register; and no register
   attached means no findings at all). Even with all four it is a rule and not a lock. This is the one
   guarantee in the fourteen where "weaker" may simply be unacceptable, and no probe can settle it:
   it is not a capability question. If the answer is no, docreview does not ship and diligence still
   does — a mis-scoped transactional finding is a commercial problem; a leaked privileged document is
   a professional-conduct problem.
4. **Whether Tier 3 re-scoped skills earn a slot beside `lq-mirror` and `lq-start`.** Seven of the
   fourteen are Tier 3: a re-scoped skill with a changed promise and, in five cases, a changed name.
   Three of them (legalquants, lq-reflect, lq-apply) sit in the same conceptual space as the two
   companion skills that already shipped, and the honest reading is that there is room for one more
   voice in that space, not three. Appetite C's bundle arithmetic forces the question anyway: the
   litigation bundle goes two over the cap of 20. The sub-decisions: does `legalquants` become a mode
   of `lq-start` (recommended) or a seventh card; does the re-scoped lq-reflect ship at all given that
   its chat-only branch standing alone is "lq-mirror with extra steps"; and does lq-apply's
   destination-free CV justify a slot against `writing`.
5. **Whether the CONTRACT section 8 no-scripts policy stays.** Both analysts assumed it throughout:
   every one of the fourteen designs is written for `exclude: scripts/**` and a host-native path for
   every step. Section 8 states the assumption plainly — "Whether bundled scripts execute is
   undocumented. Cards assume **no script runs**." Keeping it is the conservative call and costs
   nothing that these designs rely on. Changing it would need P1 to return a named interpreter and
   package set reproduced in two tenants, and would reopen closing-bible's and definition-check's
   original paths — which are the two cards whose promises the re-scope most weakens. The decision is
   worth making explicitly rather than by default, because if the policy is ever going to move, the
   two heaviest re-scopes (closing-index and definition-check, builds 3 and 12) are the work that
   would be thrown away.

Three further decisions surfaced by the merge that are not on the maintainer's original list:

6. **A licensing call on the lq-ask digest.** The dated library is LegalQuants content bundled into
   an independent adaptation. That is a rights question, separate from the external-link policy, and
   it has to be answered before anyone is commissioned to write the library.
7. **The "My LQ Moment" construct in a non-member tenant.** my-lq-moment judges against a LegalQuants
   programme's standard in bundles installed by tenants who are not members. The skill's own rules
   already forbid implying membership or certification; the maintainer should decide whether that is
   enough, or whether the rubric needs renaming.
8. **A correction to CONTRACT section 8 itself.** Section 8 currently states "Cowork cannot read
   encrypted or sensitivity-labelled files." The reader's extraction supports the first half and not
   the second: "Cowork can't read encrypted files, even if the user has access" is documented
   (gap-read C2#13), but for labelled files the only statement is "When a referenced file or email has
   a sensitivity label, Cowork shows that label and displays the highest sensitivity label for the
   session" (C2#2) — the label is displayed, not the file blocked, and whether a labelled-but-
   unencrypted file is readable is nowhere stated. Section 8 should be corrected, because docreview's
   design currently parks labelled files as unreadable on the strength of it, which may be discarding
   documents Cowork can in fact read.

## 2. How to read the tiers

**The rubric, in one paragraph.** Every step of every substitute carries an evidence letter: **D**
documented (Microsoft documents the capability, or the repo already ships a skill that does exactly
this), **U** undocumented but not denied (nothing says Cowork can; nothing says it cannot — a probe
settles it), **C** contested or unspecified (Microsoft's own pages disagree, or the capability exists
but nothing says a skill can address it, or what comes back is unspecified), **X** documented absent
or forbidden by the skill's own text. A step is load-bearing if the alternative is not worth shipping
when that step fails. **Capability risk** is the worst letter on a load-bearing step: Low is all D,
Medium has a U, High has a C or an X. **Fidelity** is one of Same deliverable / Same deliverable with
a disclosed weaker guarantee / Different deliverable / Core promise not deliverable, the last reserved
for cases where the skill's own text says a version without the missing thing is unacceptable.
**Failure mode** is Loud (the lawyer sees it fail) or Silent (they get a plausible answer that is not
backed by what it claims), recorded per U or C step with the mitigation that turns it loud. **Tier 1**
ships on documented capabilities with loud failures; **Tier 2** ships after one named probe passes, or
now with an announced degrade; **Tier 3** is a re-scoped skill with a changed promise; **Tier 4** needs
a remote MCP connector for a load-bearing step. Ruling R1 in section 6 formalises the interaction
between fidelity and tier, because the two halves of this analysis had drifted on it.

**Citation convention.** `C<page>#<quote>` refers to `gap-read-formats.md`: `C1#19` is page 1, quote
19 (the `use-cowork` Learn page); `C2` is the FAQ; `C3` the overview; `C4` what's-new; `C5` the
local-browser page; `C6` take-control-actions; `C7` manage-tasks; `C8` uses-stores; `C9`
get-started-with-cowork; `C10` start-conversation; `C11` the Learn get-started page. Skill quotes keep
their original form — "read-redline Q6" is quote 6 in that skill's section of the upstream profile
documents.

### The reader's undocumented list — what every U in this document rests on

Every U below traces to one of these. If a tester's result moves one of them, the letters that rest on
it move with it, and the reconciliation log in section 5 is the index of which.

- **Scheduling (A).** Documented: recurring *and* fixed-time scheduled prompts described in natural
  language; a Runs tab and the literal phrase "review its run history"; named triggers (an arriving
  email, a Teams channel or chat message, with an @mention filter); a cap of 25 scheduled prompts;
  "Run in" a new versus a continuing conversation; unattended execution after a one-time setup
  approval; rate limiting on event-driven tasks. **Not documented:** whether a scheduled or
  event-driven run can itself read files the user placed earlier and write outputs — implied by the
  shared Input/Output-folder model but never stated for an automated run; whether a run can be chained
  to a later run beyond "continues the current conversation"; any description of processing a large
  data room in batches; any numeric limit on event-driven tasks.
- **Inputs (B).** Documented: the full extension table (Word, Excel, PowerPoint, PDF, Markdown, Image,
  Text, Code, Config, Notebook, Audio, Video, Archive including zip); PDF and email both previewable
  and referenceable; 200 MB per attached file; "Cowork can't read encrypted files, even if the user
  has access". **Not documented, anywhere:** OCR, scanned or image-only PDFs, handwriting recognition,
  Word tracked changes, or Word comments being read. "Password-protected" is never used verbatim —
  only "encrypted" — and there is no statement on whether a labelled-but-unencrypted file is readable.
- **Outputs (C).** Documented: creation of Word, Excel, PowerPoint and PDF from scratch **and** "Edit
  and refine existing documents you share in the session"; "creates or updates files"; "creates or
  modifies files"; multi-file output as a single zip up to 50 files and 500 MB; files saved to the
  OneDrive/SharePoint workspace and to a dedicated OneDrive "Cowork" folder; preview and output
  formats including PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Markdown, code, images, CSV, **HTML** and email;
  files and folders cannot be deleted. **Not documented:** producing a Word document with tracked
  changes or comments; annotating or marking up a PDF; merging or splitting PDFs or rendering a page
  to an image; naming a specific destination folder for a task's outputs.
- **Built-in skills (D).** Documented: the fourteen-item list — Word, Excel, PowerPoint, PDF, Email,
  Scheduling, Calendar Management, Meetings, Daily Briefing, Enterprise Search, Communications,
  **Deep Research**, Adaptive Cards, App (Frontier) — repeated identically on three independent pages
  dated 8 to 14 September 2026; custom-skill limits of 50 skills, 1 MB per SKILL.md, 20 companion
  files and 10 MB total per skill; plugin skills distinguished from built-ins but stated to work the
  same way. **Not documented:** any elaboration of the PDF skill beyond the single line "Work with PDF
  documents"; "legal research" appears only as an example of what a third-party *plugin* adds, never
  as a built-in.
- **Browser use (E).** Documented: general availability to all Microsoft 365 Copilot tenants since
  August 2026 — **and off until a tenant admin turns it on**: the local-browser page (Last updated
  2026-09-16) reads "Admin control Tenant admins must enable Cowork browsing by performing the
  following steps. This feature is disabled by default." General availability means the feature is no
  longer Frontier-restricted; it does not mean it is enabled. *(This sentence sits on the same page as
  C5's other quotes but was not carried into the reader's own summary table; it is restored here
  because every U on browser use rests on it.)* Also documented: a hidden Microsoft Edge tab on the
  user's own device using their existing sign-ins,
  cookies and sessions, with "exactly the same access to sites that you have when you browse by hand —
  no more and no less"; Cowork on the web in Edge only (not other browsers, not the desktop app);
  Edge must be installed; control handed back for CAPTCHAs, credentials and MFA, and locked-account
  recovery; inherits web filtering, Conditional Access, browser-management policy and Purview DLP;
  pauses and resumes an interrupted task; captures page screenshots while it holds control. **Not
  documented:** whether content read from a page, or a file downloaded through the browser, lands
  among the session's Input or Output files.
- **Folders (F).** Documented: creating SharePoint and OneDrive folders and reorganising existing
  files into new or existing folders; browsing SharePoint and OneDrive folders in a breadcrumb tree
  view and selecting files to work with; a session's named Input folder and Output folder; folders
  cannot be deleted; custom skills live at a fixed `/Documents/Cowork/skills/` path. **Not
  documented:** that a task can be pointed at an entire folder as a single unit — selection is
  described only file by file in a tree view; any folder-listing command or output; any per-session
  limit on how many files a session can take (the 200 MB figure is per file and the 50-file/500 MB
  figure is the output zip; neither is an input-file count).

Two areas that bear on these designs were **outside this reader's brief** and are therefore not
reconciled here: whether bundled scripts execute and in what runtime (P1), and whether a
Bing-backed web search surface is addressable by a skill (P6). Letters resting on those two are
carried through unchanged and flagged as such in the reconciliation log.

## 3. Probes

P1 to P7 are as defined in `docs/research/excluded-skills-rebucket.md` and keep their numbers. The
pipeline analyst's three new probes keep the numbers they were given (P8 to P10). The companion
analyst's five are renumbered, and one of them is merged away.

**The merge.** The companion analyst's "P8 — does a bundled binary companion reach an output file?"
and the existing **P7 — can a skill's output carry a file that shipped with the skill?** test the same
mechanism: whether a companion file packaged inside a skill survives into something the lawyer
receives. They differ only in payload — an HTML template with structure to inherit versus a 583 KB PNG
that has to be embedded as bytes — and in what a pass proves. They are therefore one probe with two
arms, **P7a** and **P7b**, rather than two numbers. This matters practically: P7a can be run today for
free against the shipped `legaldesign` and `cite-check` templates, and a P7a pass with a P7b fail is
the exact result that decides whether my-lq-moment ships with its fixed cover template or with a plain
styled card. Running them as one probe on one skill package also removes the risk of two testers
attributing the same failure to two different causes.

**The renumbering map.**

| Unified | Source | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1–P6 | rebucket, unchanged | P5 narrowed by reconciliation |
| P7 (P7a / P7b) | rebucket P7 **+ companion P8** | merged; two arms |
| P8 | pipeline P8 | PDF from chosen pages |
| P9 | pipeline P9 | workbook round trip — **narrowed** by reconciliation |
| P10 | pipeline P10 | tracked changes *out* |
| P11 | companion P9 | what a skill can see and quote of its own session |
| P12 | companion P10 | browser use — **scope corrected**; keeps both arms |
| P13 | companion P11 | scheduled run over placed files — **narrowed** by reconciliation |
| P14 | companion P12 | Enterprise Search as a people finder |

Synthetic files only, one fresh conversation each, and every report should say which Cowork client was
used and what the tenant's admin posture is for web search and browser use — **including, for browser
use, whether an admin had to enable it first, since it is disabled by default**.

**One standing check that is not a probe.** On any run, the tester should note **the exact names of the
built-in skills the tenant actually offers**, as they appear in the side panel and the Sources picker,
and report the list verbatim. This settles the Deep Research question by observation rather than by
argument: Microsoft's Cowork pages name "Deep Research" while a consumer-plan support page announces
its retirement in favour of "Researcher", and those are different features sharing a name. The repo
does not rename anything — in CONTRACT section 8's list or in the four shipped cards that name it — on
the strength of a consumer notice; it renames, if ever, on the strength of a tester seeing a different
name in a real tenant.

### P1 — Does a bundled script actually run, and in what?

**Setup.** Cannot be run from the released bundles, because no shipped skill contains a script. Needs
a throwaway single-skill probe package containing `scripts/probe.py`, which prints one line naming
`sys.version`, `platform.platform()`, whether `pypdf`, `pdfplumber`, `docx`, `openpyxl` and `PIL`
import, and whether `soffice`, `pdftoppm` and `tesseract` are on the path. The SKILL.md says to run it
and report its output verbatim.

**Prompt.** `Run the environment probe and show me its output exactly as printed.`

**Pass.** The reply contains the fingerprint line with a real Python version string.

**Fail.** A reply that paraphrases the script, describes what it would print, or reports the script's
contents as instructions.

**Bad outcome shape.** **Fluent fake** — and the most informative one the programme can produce.
Record the exact wording.

**Unlocks.** Nothing directly under the no-scripts policy. It is the input to maintainer decision 5,
and to closing-bible's, definition-check's and read-redline's original (not re-scoped) paths.
Not reconciled by this reader — scripts were outside the brief.

### P2 — Does a file written in one session come back in the next?

**Setup.** None beyond two sessions on different days.

**Prompt, session 1.** `Create a file called probe-ledger.json in my Cowork Output folder containing exactly {"probe":"lqc","n":1} and tell me where you put it.`
**Prompt, a new session on a later day, with nothing attached.** `Open probe-ledger.json from my Cowork folder, tell me what n is, then save it back with n increased by one.`

**Pass.** It finds the file without the tester attaching it, reports `n` as 1, writes 2 — and a third
session reads 2.

**Fail.** It cannot find the file, or asks the tester to attach it.

**Bad outcome shape.** **Refusal**, usually — which is the safe shape. Watch for the dangerous
variant: a reply that reports `n` as 1 without having read anything, which is a fluent fake and should
be caught by making the second session's file contain `{"probe":"lqc","n":7}` instead, so a
hallucinated answer of 1 is visible.

**Unlocks.** No tier, by design. Every cross-session design in this document uses bring-your-own state
(S1b) instead, which is D today. P2 is an ergonomics upgrade for **sigpack**'s ledger,
**regulatory**'s refresh baseline, **legalquants** step 3b, **lq-reflect** step 8b and **lq-apply**
step 2b — four companion cards at once, which is why it is worth running early even though nothing
waits on it.

### P3 — Does Cowork report tracked changes in a Word document?

**Setup.** A two-page synthetic DOCX with tracked insertions and deletions by two named authors and
one comment; plus a clean copy of the same file with the changes accepted.

**Prompt.** `List every tracked change in this agreement: the exact inserted and deleted text, who made it, and where it sits. Tell me if there are none.`

**Pass.** Insertions and deletions listed separately with authors, and the comment noted; and, on the
clean copy, it says there are none rather than inventing some.

**Fail.** It reads the document as though the changes had been accepted and reports no changes.

**Bad outcome shape.** **Silent, and indistinguishable from a correct nil return** — which is exactly
why read-redline's mitigation requires the skill to quote one inserted and one deleted string with its
author before it may ever say "no tracked changes found". Run the clean-copy half; a pass on the
marked-up file with a false positive on the clean one is a different and worse result.

**Unlocks.** **read-redline Tier 2 → shippable without a banner.** Also strengthens the shipped
`playbook-review`, which disclaims tracked-changes output today. Rests on B-undocumented: Word tracked
changes are nowhere mentioned in Microsoft's pages.

### P4 — Can Cowork tell what is on a page?

**Setup.** A four-page synthetic PDF: page 2 carries redline markup (strikethrough and underline),
page 3 is a scanned image of a signature block signed over a printed name, page 4 is clean.

**Prompt.** `Go through this PDF page by page. For each page tell me whether anything is struck through or underlined, whether there is handwriting or a signature, and whether the page is scanned rather than typed.`

**Pass.** Correct per-page answers, including that page 3 is an image and is signed, without being
told.

**Fail.** It describes pages it cannot see, or reports the scanned page as blank or as text.

**Bad outcome shape.** **Fluent fake, and the most costly in the pipeline half** — a model that
believes it has looked at a scanned signature page and has not will report a signed block that is
blank.

**Unlocks.** **read-redline's PDF path**; how many documents **diligence** and **docreview** park as
unreadable; **closing-index**'s scanned execution pages; **regulatory**'s handling of a scanned
official PDF; and it is the first of the two things **sigpack 2B** needs. Rests on B-undocumented: OCR,
scanned PDFs and handwriting are nowhere mentioned.

### P5 — What does a resumed task remember?

**Setup.** Two sessions plus one resumption, over two days.

**Prompt, session 1.** `Remember this reference for later: ALDERNEY-7. Draft me two lines about anything.`
**Then, resuming that task from the task list the next day.** `What was the reference I gave you?`
**Then, in a brand-new task.** `What did I ask you in my previous Cowork task?`

**Pass.** The resumed task returns ALDERNEY-7 unprompted; the new task does not know.

**Fail.** The resumed task does not have it, or — the bigger finding — the *new* task does.

**Bad outcome shape.** **Fluent fake** on the third prompt. A brand-new task that produces a plausible
account of the previous one should be reported loudly.

**Unlocks.** No tier. It bounds **lq-reflect** Alternative B, which needs the lawyer's whole week
across every session rather than one thread. **[reconciled]** Narrowed: the *mechanism* of continuation
is now documented — "Run in: Whether each run starts a new conversation or continues the current one"
(C1#25) and "Conversation and task history: Your prompts and Copilot's responses are saved so you can
review past tasks" (C8#1) — so P5 no longer asks whether resumption exists. It asks only what
re-enters the model's context when it happens, which remains nowhere stated.

### P6 — Is web search on in this tenant, and what comes back?

**Setup.** None.

**Prompt.** `Search the web and quote me, word for word, the opening sentence of the official text you find at legislation.gov.uk for the Bribery Act 2010 section 1, and give me the address of the page you read.`

**Pass.** A URL and a quotation, plus whatever the client says about web search.

**Fail.** A refusal naming a disabled web-search setting — which is an equally useful result and
should be reported with the tenant's admin posture if known.

**Bad outcome shape.** **Fluent fake** — a quotation with no address, or an address that was not read.
This is the one probe where a "pass" can be worse than a fail: a quotation sourced from a rendering
the lawyer cannot check is exactly what **regulatory**'s central rule forbids.

**Unlocks.** No tier. It confirms a limit rather than lifting one. It informs **lq-ask**'s optional
step 9 and **regulatory**'s locator-only use of search, and it matters because the shipped `cite-check`
assumes a research surface. Not reconciled by this reader — search was outside the brief.

### P7 — Can a skill's output carry a file that shipped with the skill?

Two arms, one probe. **[reconciled]** Carrying the asset is now documented: "Skills can also include
up to 20 companion files (such as reference documents and scripts), with a total of 10 MB per skill"
(C1#17). What P7 tests is whether a carried asset reaches an output, which is nowhere stated.

**P7a — an HTML/text template.** Setup: none; run against the shipped `legaldesign` skill, which
carries HTML templates as companions. Prompt:
`Turn the attached advice note into a one-pager using your supplied template, and tell me which of your template files you used.`
Pass: the produced HTML visibly uses the packaged template's structure and the reply names the
companion file. Fail: generic HTML, or a named file that was not used.

**P7b — a bundled binary image.** Setup: package a probe skill carrying a small PNG and an HTML
template that references it. Prompt:
`Build the card from your supplied template and tell me which of your packaged files it used.`
Pass: the rendered page shows the image and the reply names the file. Fail, recorded separately: the
page renders without the image, or the reply claims an image that is not there.

**Bad outcome shape.** P7a: **fluent fake** (a named template that was not used). P7b: usually
**loud** — a missing image in a preview pane is visible — with a fluent-fake variant where the reply
asserts the image is present.

**Unlocks.** **my-lq-moment**'s cover keeps its fixed template (P7b) rather than degrading to a plain
styled card; **read-redline**'s and **docreview**'s HTML views and **definition-check**'s review page
all rest on P7a. P7a is free today and should be the first probe the programme runs.

### P8 — Can Cowork build a PDF out of chosen pages of supplied PDFs?

**Setup.** Two synthetic four-page PDFs with distinguishable page content.

**Prompt.** `Make me one new PDF containing page 3 of the first document and pages 1 and 2 of the second, in that order, and tell me how many pages it has.`

**Pass.** A downloadable PDF in the Output folder with exactly those three pages in that order and
their content intact.

**Fail.** A reply that describes the pages instead of producing the file, or a PDF with the wrong
pages or order.

**Bad outcome shape.** **Fluent fake** in its most checkable form — the page count is stated in the
reply and the file is right there, so a tester catches it in seconds. Still record it, because the
claim came back confident.

**Unlocks.** **sigpack 2B**'s pack and compile halves; **closing-index**'s combined PDF. Rests on
C-undocumented: merging or splitting PDFs is nowhere mentioned, and the PDF built-in's entire
description is the single line "Work with PDF documents" (C1#14).

### P9 — Does an Excel workbook survive a round trip with its arithmetic intact?

**[reconciled]** Narrowed. The round trip itself is documented: "Edit and refine existing documents
you share in the session" (C3#4), "When Cowork creates or updates files during a session" (C1#2),
"When Cowork creates or modifies files during a conversation" (C6#2). What is nowhere documented is
whether the *structure and the formulas* survive, so P9 now tests only that.

**Setup.** None; two sessions.

**Prompt, session 1.** `Build me a tracker workbook with columns Document, Issue, Status, Quote, Locator and these six rows, and add a row at the bottom that counts how many rows are filled.`
**Prompt, a fresh session with the returned workbook attached.** `Add these four documents as new rows, fill the Status column for them, and give the workbook back to me unchanged apart from the new rows.`

**Pass.** The returned file keeps the original six rows, the column order, the count formula **as a
formula**, and its recalculated value.

**Fail.** Rows dropped or reordered, columns renamed, or the count arriving as a hard-coded number
rather than a live formula.

**Bad outcome shape.** **Silent** — a re-emitted workbook that lost the formula looks exactly like a
workbook. Mitigation for every design that uses the register: the skill prints the row count it read
and the row count it wrote, so a drop is visible in the reply and not only in the file.

**Unlocks.** The register pattern (S15) that carries state and coverage arithmetic for **diligence**,
**docreview**, **closing-index** and **definition-check**, and **sigpack**'s ledger. After P3 this is
the probe with the widest reach in the pipeline half.

### P10 — Can Cowork produce a Word document carrying real tracked changes?

**Setup.** A short supplied agreement clause.

**Prompt.** `Give me back this clause as a Word document with my three edits recorded as tracked changes attributed to "Review", not as plain edited text.`

**Pass.** Opening the file in Word shows insertions and deletions in the review pane.

**Fail.** A document with the edits applied as plain text, or coloured text that is not a tracked
change.

**Bad outcome shape.** **Loud** — the lawyer opens the file and sees immediately. This is the
comfortable kind of probe.

**Unlocks.** **read-redline**'s annotated Word copy (step 8b) and **clause-fit**'s redline (step 7).
Neither is load-bearing; both improve. Rests on C-undocumented: producing a Word document with tracked
changes or comments is nowhere stated.

### P11 — What can a skill see and quote of its own session?

**Setup.** In one conversation, do some work first: type a distinctive sentence, have a file created,
invoke a second skill.

**Prompt.** `Quote back, word for word, the sentence I typed at the start of this session; list the files created in this session; and list the skills and tools used. Say for each whether you are reading it or reconstructing it.`

**Pass.** A verbatim quote, the correct file list, the correct skill and tool list, each with honest
attribution.

**Fail, in two distinguishable ways.** A refusal (usable — the cards adapt around it) or a fluent
paraphrase presented as a quote.

**Bad outcome shape.** **Fluent fake, and the worst in this document.** A model asked "how did that
go?" will produce an account of a session with quotes that read like quotes whether or not it can see
anything. The second fail mode is the reason lq-reflect waits on this probe rather than shipping
alongside it.

**Unlocks.** The hinge of **lq-reflect**'s alternative (step 6 — "A moment with no quote is not a
moment; drop it") and step 4 of **my-lq-moment**'s. A refusal result does not block either: it forces
every quote to come from an attached file, which narrows the audience but keeps both skills honest.
Note what is already documented and does not need probing: that loaded skills appear in the side panel
for the *user* (C1#13) — nothing says a *skill* can enumerate them, which is why step 4 stays C.

### P12 — Browser use: does what it reads reach the skill?

**[reconciled]** Scope corrected, not narrowed — **P12 keeps both arms.** The route is documented:
browser use "moved from Frontier to general availability to all Microsoft 365 Copilot tenants"
(C4#2), needs Edge installed, runs only in Cowork on the web in Edge (never the desktop app, never
another browser), and inherits web filtering, Conditional Access and Purview DLP (C5#3, C5#6). But it
is **off until an admin turns it on** — the same page reads "Admin control Tenant admins must enable
Cowork browsing by performing the following steps. This feature is disabled by default." So the probe
tests two things: **(a) availability** — is it enabled in this tenant, and what did it take to enable
it; and **(b) quotability** — does page content or a downloaded file become something the session,
and therefore a skill, can quote. (b) is the arm no design can route around; (a) is the arm that keeps
every browser step at **U** for availability however (b) turns out.

**Setup.** Cowork on the web in Microsoft Edge, on a device where Edge is installed, in a tenant where
an admin has performed the enable steps. Record what the posture was before the tester asked, who had
to act, and the tenant's DLP and site-blocking posture. A tenant where the admin declines is a
reportable result, not a failed run.

**Prompt.** `Open the page at <public URL> and quote its first heading and the first sentence under it. Tell me how you retrieved it, and tell me whether that page is now one of my session files.`

**Pass for these designs.** The quote comes back, Cowork names the route, and it states plainly
whether the content is now a session file.

**Fail.** The content comes back as a summary rather than quotable text; or a refusal citing a blocked
site (still a useful result — record it with the posture).

**Bad outcome shape.** **Fluent fake** — a "quotation" that is the model's rendering of the page
rather than its text. This is precisely what **regulatory**'s central rule forbids quoting, so a
pass here does not license regulatory to use it.

**Unlocks.** **lq-connect** Alternative C; **lq-apply** step 5's handle verification (a capability
question that is separately a policy question); **lq-ask**'s optional step. No design in this document
depends on it.

### P13 — Can a scheduled run work over files placed earlier?

**[reconciled]** Narrowed. The scheduling machinery is documented and can be described to the lawyer
with confidence: recurring or fixed-time prompts set up in natural language (C1#19, C7#4), a Runs tab
and "review its run history" (C1#21, C1#26), event triggers on an arriving email or Teams message
including @mentions (C1#23), unattended execution after a one-time setup approval (C6#3), and a hard
cap of 25 scheduled prompts (C1#22). What is nowhere stated is whether an automated run can read files
the user placed earlier and write outputs, and whether a custom skill is invoked inside one.

**Setup.** A file already sitting in the Cowork folder, and a custom skill installed.

**Prompt.** Schedule a task that names the custom skill and the placed file — for example
`Every Monday at 9am, use the <name> skill on closing-register.xlsx in my Cowork folder and save the updated register back.`

**Pass.** The Runs tab shows it ran; an output file appears; the output reflects the placed file; and
the run history shows the custom skill was loaded.

**Fail.** The schedule exists but the skill is not invoked, or nothing is written, or the run reports
success with no output.

**Bad outcome shape.** **Silent** — a schedule that runs and produces nothing looks exactly like a
schedule that has not run yet. Check the Runs tab, not the Output folder.

**Unlocks.** S14 for every cadence-shaped idea in both halves: **lq-reflect**'s weekly debrief,
**regulatory**'s refresh, **legalquants**'s check-in, and **diligence**/**docreview**'s tranche
batches. No card in this document is built on it; all of them may offer it as a closing line, and
must name the 25-prompt cap when they do.

### P14 — Enterprise Search as a people finder

**Setup.** A tenant with real internal material behind the chosen topic.

**Prompt.** `Who in my organisation has worked on <a topic with real internal material behind it>? Name up to three people and, for each, quote the document that shows it.`

**Pass.** Named people, each with a quotable document.

**Fail.** Documents with no people attached, or names with nothing behind them.

**Bad outcome shape.** **Fluent fake** — three plausible colleagues, padded to three because the
question asked for three. lq-connect's mitigation is upstream's own rule kept verbatim: "Pick two or
three — fewer when that is what the evidence supports, never pad", plus a requirement to quote, for
each person, the document that put them on the list.

**Unlocks.** The load-bearing step of **lq-connect** Alternative A. Enterprise Search is documented as
a built-in by exact name — "Enterprise Search Search across your organization" (C1#15) — and that one
line is the entirety of what Microsoft says about it, which is why what it returns is a U.

## 4. Substitution pattern catalogue

Eighteen patterns. S1 to S14 are carried from the companion analysis, with the letters and probe
numbers reconciled and renumbered; S15 to S18 are the pipeline analysis's patterns, written up in the
same shape. Each says what it is, what it substitutes for, its evidence letter, the probe that settles
it, its failure mode, and which of the fourteen skills use it.

### S1. The private store, four ways

**Substitutes for:** `~/.lq/profile.json`, `journey.jsonl`, `scan_state.json`, `state.json`, the
`onboarding.json` marker — everything upstream keeps on the lawyer's own disk between runs.

- **S1a — a named file in the Output folder.** The skill writes `lq-journey-2026-09-19.md` (or
  `.json`) and says where it is. **Write: D** — "You can also access files that Cowork creates
  directly in your OneDrive Cowork folder at any time" (C1#4), and the session has a named Output
  folder (C1#9) **[reconciled — quote numbers added]**. **Read it back next session without the
  lawyer attaching it: U** — nothing documents it, nothing denies it; what *is* documented is the user
  selecting a file from a tree view (C1#12), which is the opposite of unprompted read-back.
  **Probe: P2.** **Failure mode: Silent** if a card assumes the read-back and the file quietly is not
  there, because the model will carry on from an empty state and sound exactly as confident.
  **Mitigation:** the card must state the file it expected and what it did not find, in the reply,
  before it does anything else. **Used by:** lq-reflect (the debrief note), lq-apply (the evidence
  file), legalquants (the journey note), my-lq-moment (assets only), sigpack's ledger, regulatory's
  refresh baseline, clause-fit's retired ledger, closing-index's register.
- **S1b — bring your own state.** The lawyer attaches the state file at the start of each session.
  **D**, and not theoretical: `playbook-review` ships this today — "Require an approved playbook: the
  `playbook.md` a build produced, or one the lawyer supplies in the same shape... There is no library
  to search and no registry to discover; if the lawyer does not know where theirs is, ask them to
  point at the folder." Paired with S1a it gives a genuine cross-session loop with no undocumented
  step in it: one skill writes the file, the lawyer keeps it, the next session they attach it.
  **Failure mode: Loud** — a missing attachment is visible to both parties. **Used by:** lq-reflect,
  lq-apply, legalquants, and every pipeline skill that wanted a ledger. **This is the pattern the
  whole document turns on: bring-your-own beat cross-session read-back in every design in both
  halves.** See ruling R3 for what a skill does when the state is absent.
- **S1c — `lqplaybook.md` as the carrier for confirmed preferences.** Per CONTRACT section 8 and the
  fourteen shipped cards that already do it (every skill in both bundles except `lq-start`,
  `lq-mirror` and `wiki`): read only confirmed `[skill-name]` entries from a playbook the lawyer
  attached or placed in the Input folder, never write it, and offer a discovered preference as one
  exact line they can add. **D** — the Input folder is documented (C1#9). It carries settled
  preferences well and a journey badly — it is a preferences file, not an event log, and widening its
  tag space to a new skill name is a contract edit, not a capability question. **Failure mode: Loud.**
  **Used by:** legalquants (the quoting posture, the declined move), lq-apply (format preference),
  lq-reflect (the one change in play, at a stretch), my-lq-moment (nothing kept, or one line offered).
- **S1d — an Excel workbook as the journey, via the Excel built-in.** **Produce: D.** **[reconciled]
  Update in place across sessions: split.** Editing a workbook the lawyer shares in the session is now
  **D** — "Edit and refine existing documents you share in the session" (C3#4), "creates or updates
  files" (C1#2), "creates or modifies files" (C6#2). What remains **U** is (i) reading it back without
  the lawyer attaching it (P2) and (ii) whether the returned workbook keeps its structure and live
  formulas (**P9**, narrowed). **Failure mode: Silent** — a re-emitted workbook that drops earlier
  rows or hard-codes the count looks like a workbook. **Mitigation:** the skill prints the row count
  it read and the row count it wrote. **Used by:** lq-reflect, lq-apply; and as S15 in the pipeline
  half by diligence, docreview, closing-index, definition-check and sigpack.

### S2. The Output folder as the working directory

**Substitutes for:** run directories, scratch paths, intermediates that upstream deletes.
**Evidence: D** — the session has a named Input folder and Output folder with Download and Preview
buttons per file (C1#9, C6#2) — with one documented constraint: "Cowork can't delete files or folders
in OneDrive or SharePoint" (C2#13, C8#6), so nothing may be described as cleaned up.
**[reconciled] Two precisions.** First, *creating* a folder is documented — "Create SharePoint and
OneDrive folders. Reorganize your existing files into new or existing folders" (C3#4) — so a dated run
folder per matter is available as an explicit step, and the designs that flattened run folders to
filenames did so on an unstated assumption rather than a documented limit. What is **not** documented
is naming a destination folder for a task's outputs at write time (C-undocumented), so outputs land in
the Cowork folder and are moved afterwards if the lawyer wants them elsewhere. Second, *modifying*
cloud storage is approval-gated rather than impossible — "Account altering actions, including deleting
files, modifying cloud storage..." require permission (C6#4) — which is a stronger statement than
"Cowork cannot modify", and matters to diligence's claim that its sources are untouchable.
**Probe:** none needed. **Failure mode: Loud** (the files are visibly there). **Used by:** all
fourteen. Shipped precedent in cite-check, legaldesign, closing-checklist, playbook-review,
client-update, document-discovery and pressuretest, each of which now says which working files remain
and names them.

**The output zip, unused by both analysts and worth having.** "The archive can include up to 50 files
and 500 MB total" (C1#3, C2#12). That is a documented way to hand a lawyer a whole tranche's outputs
in one download — and it is also a real cap on how large a tranche's output set may be, which
diligence, docreview, closing-index and signature-pages should each state when they size a tranche.

### S3. The script re-expressed as prose

**Substitutes for:** every bundled script whose job was deterministic bookkeeping or formatting rather
than computation the model cannot do. **Evidence: D** for the prose path itself; **C** for keeping the
script as an optional accelerator, because Microsoft's pages disagree about whether `scripts/` runs and
no page names an interpreter (**probe: P1**; not reconciled here — scripts were outside this reader's
brief). Repo policy is that cards ship no scripts, revisited per card only if P1 returns a named
interpreter and package set reproduced in two tenants — maintainer decision 5. **Failure mode of the
prose path: Silent** where the script enforced a rule the prose merely states — a refusal, a length
cap, a check. **[ruling R4]** Every such self-enforced rule is Silent by default and needs a visible
output to become Loud: write the rule as a step with something the lawyer reads ("I shortened this to
nine words; here it is"), not as a background guarantee. **Used by:** all fourteen. Shipped precedent:
`wiki` (`wiki.py` became "read the delimited block out of the note itself and quote it exactly"),
`lq-start` (`catalog.py` became a static map), `legaldesign` (`scaffold.py` became "Build it yourself,
in this order, and do not skip a step because the next one looks close"), `playbook-builder` ("What
replaces validation is reading").

### S4. Renderers

**Substitutes for:** PNG output from an SVG rasteriser shelling out to local binaries.

- An HTML page for the preview pane, built from a bundled template: **D** for HTML rendering —
  "Supported formats include PDF, Microsoft 365 documents (Word, Excel, PowerPoint), Markdown, code
  files, images, CSV, HTML, and email" (C11#3) **[reconciled — HTML confirmed by name]**; **U** for
  whether a bundled asset actually appears in the output (**P7a** for an HTML template, **P7b** for a
  binary image).
- A slide via the PowerPoint built-in: **D** — "PowerPoint Create and edit PowerPoint presentations"
  (C1#14).
- The lawyer converts: **D**, and blessed by the skill's own text in the one case that needs it
  (my-lq-moment Q9).

**Failure mode: Silent and severe if the model draws the image itself or claims a PNG exists** —
my-lq-moment Q9 forbids both by name. **Mitigation:** carry Q9's prohibition into the adapted body
verbatim. **Used by:** my-lq-moment (the cover), read-redline (the marked-passage view, S18),
definition-check (the review page), docreview (the findings page), diligence (the crosswalk),
closing-index (the index page). **Not available:** rendering a PDF page to an image is nowhere
documented (C-undocumented), which is what sigpack 2B's visual gate would have needed.

### S5. Network reach

**Substitutes for:** live fetches of a corpus, a member directory, a public site, a repository.

- A declared `agentConnectors` remote MCP server: **D** as a route (Streamable HTTP, JSON-RPC 2.0,
  OAuth or dynamic client registration, up to 10 per package, `copilot-cowork/1.0` on outbound
  requests). It is Tier 4 because somebody has to operate the server.
- A dated static snapshot shipped as companions: **D**, and stale by construction — and now costed
  against a documented budget of 20 companion files and 10 MB per skill (C1#17). See **ruling R2** for
  when a snapshot caps a skill's tier and when it does not.
- Browser use reading a public page: **[reconciled] D as a route; U for availability in any given
  tenant.** The route is documented: it "moved from Frontier to general availability to all Microsoft
  365 Copilot tenants" (C4#2); the tab runs in the user's own Edge with their sign-ins and "exactly
  the same access to sites that you have when you browse by hand — no more and no less" (C5#2); it
  hands control back for CAPTCHAs, credentials and MFA (C5#5); it inherits web filtering, Conditional
  Access and Purview DLP (C5#6). The documented preconditions are real and must be stated rather than
  assumed: Edge installed, Cowork on the web, never the desktop app (C5#3) — **and an admin who has
  turned it on**, because the local-browser page reads "Admin control Tenant admins must enable Cowork
  browsing by performing the following steps. This feature is disabled by default." General
  availability is not the same as enabled by default, and **a step whose availability depends on an
  admin toggle stays U for availability**. So two Us, not one: whether it is on in this tenant, and
  whether what the browser reads becomes quotable session content (**P12** tests both). No card may
  depend on either.
- Bing-backed web search: **C** — the article says it applies to Cowork, Cowork has no user toggle, an

  not reconciled — search was outside this reader's brief).
- Bring your own page — the lawyer pastes or attaches it: **D**.

**Failure mode of the snapshot: Silent staleness.** **Mitigation:** the date is in the first line of
every answer and in the description, and the card is forbidden from claiming currency. **Failure mode
of search and browser use: mixed** — a refusal is loud, a confident answer sourced from a rendering
the lawyer cannot check is silent. **Mitigation:** name the route used and the fact that it is a
rendering, never bytes. **Used by:** lq-ask, lq-connect, lq-apply (the form fields and the handle),
legalquants (the Residency line), regulatory (which refuses a rendering by rule), cite-check (which
ships today and assumes a research surface).

### S6. Session transcripts across sessions

**Substitutes for:** reading the lawyer's own past AI sessions off disk over a window.

- The lawyer attaches previous sessions' Output files, or a running journey file they keep: **D**.
- A resumed task, where the current conversation is itself a past session: **[reconciled] the
  mechanism is D; what it carries is U.** Continuation is documented — "Run in: Whether each run
  starts a new conversation or continues the current one" (C1#25) and "Conversation and task history:
  Your prompts and Copilot's responses are saved so you can review past tasks" (C8#1) — but no page
  says what re-enters the model's context (**probe: P5**, narrowed, with **P7** on the adjacent
  question).
- Current-session-only mode: **D** — "Plugin skills work within the current conversation context. They
  can read files you attached, reference earlier messages."
- The lawyer pastes what they remember: **D**.

**Failure mode: Silent** — a model asked about last week will produce a plausible account of last week
from the conversation it is in. This is the single most dangerous substitution in the set, because the
output looks identical whether the evidence existed or not. **Mitigation:** the card states the
evidence it actually read, by name, before any finding, and lq-reflect's own coverage discipline
("Coverage is always stated: the files read, their dates, messages omitted or shortened") is kept
verbatim rather than adapted. **Probe: P11** for what a skill can quote of its own session — the
sharper question, and the one lq-reflect waits on. **Used by:** lq-reflect above all; my-lq-moment

### S8. The installed-plugin scan

### S9. The argument grammar

### S11. Promote the skill's own documented degrade to the whole skill

**Substitutes for:** inventing a new fallback. Five of the six companions, and four of the eight
pipeline skills, already contain a written, first-class path for the case where the machinery is
absent, and in every case it is closer to Cowork than anything an adapter would invent:

- lq-ask Q5: "If it is **not available**: say so in one plain line ('the live corpus isn't reachable

- lq-apply Q9: "**Empty or missing store:** say so honestly and start one now... then compile from what

- lq-reflect Q9: "If they decline, work only from what they tell you — that is often enough."
- my-lq-moment Q9: hand over the file and the approved text and give the one-line conversion; and its

- lq-reflect step 8: "Without workers, skip this and say so in one line; the debrief is complete

- read-redline Q6: "If the host cannot render pages, say plainly that visual reconciliation did not run

- definition-check Q6: "Analyze only content actually exposed by the host. Request pasted/exported text

only branch, which is why several designs end at Tier 3 rather than Tier 1 — see ruling R1.

### S13. Hand off to a built-in instead of to a bundled program

### S14. A scheduled task as the cadence

**Substitutes for:** "run this every week", which upstream got from the lawyer's own habit and a

### S15. The Excel register as receipt

**Substitutes for:** the hash-balanced receipt script, the run directory that held state between
passes, and the coverage certification. One workbook does three jobs at once: it is the state the
lawyer carries between sessions (S1b), it is the reconciliation, and its arithmetic is inspectable.
One row per unit of work — per expected closing item, per document × issue, per defined term — with a
status column, the verbatim quote, the locator, and a visible count row whose formula the lawyer can
read. **Evidence:** producing it **D** (C1#14, "Excel Create and edit Excel spreadsheets"); handing it
back updated when the lawyer attaches it **D [reconciled]** (C3#4, C1#2, C6#2); **U** on whether the
structure and the live formula survive the round trip (**P9**, narrowed) and **U** on read-back
without attaching (**P2**). **Failure mode: Silent in the model, Loud in the workbook** — and that
substitution is the whole point. A model that says "all 43 items are accounted for" cannot be checked;
43 rows with a status column and a visible count formula can be checked in ten seconds. Its honesty
comes from being inspectable, not from being computed correctly. **Mitigation:** the skill prints the
row count it read and the row count it wrote, and never uses the word receipt, certified or proved
(maintainer decision 2). **Used by:** closing-index (the status register), diligence (the master
register), docreview (the same, with privilege as column one), definition-check (the definitions
register), signature-pages (the ledger), lq-reflect and lq-apply (S1d, the lighter form).

### S16. Tranche processing

**Substitutes for:** the full-room scaled pass that ran across a run directory with a state file.
The room is worked a tranche at a time, one session per tranche, each tranche's rows appended to the
register the lawyer attaches at the start of the next session. The method — the framework, the gates,
the quote rule, the second pass — is identical in every tranche; what changes is that the boundary is
stated on every delivery. **Evidence:** the per-session work **D**; **[reconciled] the tranche size is
U, not a documented cap** — Microsoft states a 200 MB per-file limit (C2#13) and a 50-file/500 MB
*output-zip* limit (C1#3), and no per-session input-file count anywhere (F-undocumented), so the card
must have the lawyer choose the tranche rather than infer a ceiling that is not there. Attaching a
whole folder as a unit is also **U** (F-undocumented): selection is documented only file by file in a
tree view (C1#12). Running a tranche on a schedule is **U** (**P13**). **Failure mode: Loud** — no
register attached, no continuation, and the skill says which tranches are recorded and which are not.
The silent risk sits one level up, in the coverage claim, and is handled by S15. **Mitigation:** the
tranche boundary is stated on every delivery, not only at the end. **Used by:** diligence, docreview,
closing-index, and sigpack 2B's scan.

### S17. The provenance block

**Substitutes for:** a retrieval receipt — the fetch that refused off-host redirects, timestamped the
retrieval and hashed the bytes. The lawyer goes to the official publisher, downloads the document and
places it in the Input folder; the skill prints a block naming the publisher, the address, the date
retrieved and the lawyer's own statement that they took it from there, and the block travels with
every deliverable alongside the source filename. The bytes are genuinely the publisher's; what changes
is who attests to that. **Evidence: D** — the lawyer's upload is documented (C1#10, C10#1) and the
Input folder is named (C1#9); the hash and the redirect refusal are **X**, having no analogue.
**Probe:** none. **P6** bears only on using search to *locate* the right document, never to quote it.
**Failure mode: Loud, and the skill already owns the mechanism** — an incomplete provenance block
drops the answer to regulatory Q9's "Your answer — provisional and unverified" heading with the
unproved points named. **Used by:** regulatory (the whole design), and in weaker forms by lq-ask
(citation by title and date, never described as live) and lq-connect (the tenant document quoted as
the ground for a match). The pattern generalises: **where a guarantee cannot be computed, name who is
standing behind it.**

### S18. The HTML marked-passage view

**Substitutes for:** annotating the lawyer's own PDF and handing it back with marks on it. In its
place, a generated HTML page for the preview pane showing each changed passage old-beside-new with its
theme and materiality tier, or each finding beside its quote and locator. **Evidence: D** for
producing and previewing HTML — "Supported formats include PDF, Microsoft 365 documents (Word, Excel,
PowerPoint), Markdown, code files, images, CSV, HTML, and email" (C11#3) **[reconciled — HTML
confirmed by name]**; **U** for whether a bundled template's assets reach the page (**P7a**, and
**P7b** if it carries an image); **U, not X [reconciled]** for the thing it replaces — annotating a

Loud** — the page is either in the preview pane or it is not. The loss is loud too, and must be stated

diligence (the crosswalk), closing-index (the index page), my-lq-moment (the cover card).

## 6. Consistency rulings

Where the same situation drew different treatment in the two halves, one treatment is chosen here and
applied throughout section 7. Each ruling cites the step that differs, and none of them flattens a
real difference.

### R1 — Fidelity and tier are separate axes, and the tier test is mechanical

**The drift.** lq-ask Alternative A is rated **"Fidelity: Different deliverable"** and **Tier 2**.
Every other design in both halves with "Different deliverable" is Tier 3: signature-pages, clause-fit,
legalquants, lq-connect A, lq-apply. Meanwhile regulatory A is **Tier 1** with "Same deliverable with a
disclosed weaker guarantee", and definition-check is **Tier 2** with the identical fidelity line — so
fidelity alone clearly does not fix the tier either.

**The ruling.** The tier is set by three tests applied in order, and the fidelity line is an input to
only the third.

1. **Did the promise change?** If fidelity is "Different deliverable" or "Core promise not
   deliverable", it is **Tier 3** — a re-scoped skill, with a changed name where the old one would
   mislead. Nothing below this test can rescue it.
2. **Otherwise, is a load-bearing step non-D?** If yes, it is **Tier 2**, and the tier is discharged
   when the named probe passes.
3. **Otherwise, are all failures Loud?** If yes, **Tier 1**. If a characteristic failure is Silent, it
   is **Tier 2** with an announced degrade, however clean the capability picture.

**What moves.** **lq-ask Alternative A becomes Tier 3** (from Tier 2). Its own analysis says the live
fetch is the promise — Q3's rooms "searched live" and Q4's "fetched live" are named as unavailable in
every answer — and step 3 replaces the corpus with a dated digest. That is a changed promise and needs
a changed description, which is Tier 3 by test 1. **definition-check stays Tier 2** even though the
reconciliation moved its capability risk to Low, because test 3 catches it: its characteristic failure
(an occurrence the model did not notice) is Silent. **regulatory stays Tier 1** on the same test, in
the other direction: its characteristic failure is the skill's own "provisional and unverified"
heading, which the lawyer reads.

**Consequence for the appetite scenarios.** Under Appetite B, lq-ask does not ship. That is the
honest result: it was the one design whose tier was carrying a changed promise without saying so.

### R2 — A dated snapshot caps the tier only when it is the evidence

**The comparison the brief asks for.** lq-ask ships a static dated companion library (step 3) and is
tiered on its staleness. regulatory ships dated jurisdiction registries (step 1) and is Tier 1
notwithstanding that they "are a dated static snapshot and go stale".

**The ruling: both stand, and the citable difference is what the snapshot does.** In regulatory, the
registry is a **pointer** — step 1 uses `references/jurisdictions/` to tell the lawyer which publisher
to go to, and the authority quoted at step 6 is the publisher's own document that the lawyer placed in
the Input folder at step 3. A stale registry sends the lawyer to a moved page, which fails **Loud**:
they go looking and either find it or they do not. In lq-ask, the digest at step 3 **is** the evidence
that step 7 cites; nothing fresher is ever consulted. A stale digest fails **Silent**: a six-month-old
answer reads exactly like a current one.

**The rule to apply uniformly.** A dated snapshot that only routes the user to a live source does not
cap the skill's tier; it carries an "as at" line and nothing more. A dated snapshot that is itself the
evidence caps the skill at Tier 2 at best, and its date must appear in the description, in the first
line of every answer, and in the closing scope statement, with the card forbidden from words that
imply currency ("currently", "the community now thinks", "the latest position").

**What moves.** regulatory's jurisdiction registries must carry the dated "as at" line the analyst
already prescribed — that part was already right — **and** the same forbidden-vocabulary rule, which
was not. lq-ask's date discipline is unchanged. lq-ask's tier moves for a different reason (R1).

### R3 — Bring-your-own state: one rule for what happens when the state is absent

**The drift.** Bring-your-own state is rated D and Loud in both halves, and no design tiers on it —
that much was already consistent. What was not consistent is what a skill *does* when the file is not
there. Four different behaviours: **docreview step 6** refuses to produce any findings at all;
**diligence step 7** states which tranches are recorded and continues; **legalquants step 3** asks one
question and continues; **lq-apply step 2b** names the file it looked for and continues.

**The ruling: one rule, graded by what the state protects.**

- Where the carried state holds a **legal hold or a scope claim** — docreview's privilege column is the
  only instance in the fourteen — its absence is a **hard stop**. No register, no findings. This is not
  caution; it is the only thing that converts a silent state dependency into a loud one, and it is the
  reason docreview is shippable at all.
- Where the carried state holds **continuity only** — diligence's tranche history, lq-reflect's kept
  change, lq-apply's evidence, legalquants' journey, sigpack's ledger, closing-index's register,
  regulatory's refresh baseline — its absence is **named in the first reply, before any finding**, the
  skill states the reduced scope it is now working in, and it continues.

In both cases the card must name the file it expected and say it did not find it, in the reply, before
it does anything else. The difference is only what happens next.

### R4 — A self-performed check is Silent by default

**The comparison the brief asks for.** **read-redline step 2b** — the calibration gate the skill
applies to itself — is rated **Silent**. **my-lq-moment step 5** — the insufficient-evidence rule the
skill applies to itself — is rated **Loud**. Both are "the skill enforces its own gate", and the
halves disagreed.

**The ruling: Silent by default, with one test that makes it Loud.** *Could the run have produced a
deliverable that looks the same with the check skipped?* If yes, the failure is **Silent** and the
mitigation must be a visible output the lawyer reads in the same reply. If no — if the check's only
possible outcome is a refusal or a printed block — it is **Loud**.

**What that decides.**

- read-redline step 2b: **Silent.** A calibration that was never confirmed still produces a confident
  report. Mitigation, as designed: the calibration line and the lawyer's confirmation are reproduced
  verbatim at the head of the deliverable, so an unconfirmed run is visible on the artifact.
- my-lq-moment step 5: **Loud**, and it stands. The gate's only outcome is a refusal the lawyer reads.
- my-lq-moment step 11 (the skill counts the twelve words itself): **Silent**, reclassified from
  unlabelled. Mitigation, which S3 already prescribes: show the shortened sentence — "I shortened this
  to nine words; here it is."
- diligence step 8 and docreview step 7 (a second pass described as verification): **Silent** unless
  the execution shape is recorded and the skill declines the word "independent". Stands.
- regulatory step 7 (the skill re-locates its own quotes): **Silent**, with an unusually strong
  mitigation — the source file is in the lawyer's own Input folder, every quote carries its provision
  label, and checking one takes ten seconds. Stands.
- read-redline step 5 and every register count: **Silent in the model, Loud in the workbook** (S15).
  Stands, and it is the design move the whole pipeline half rests on.

### R5 — A mode inherits the tier of the card that carries it

**The drift.** lq-connect **Alternative B** (bring your own directory) is rated **Low** capability
risk, "Same deliverable with a disclosed weaker guarantee", failure **Loud** — which is Tier 1 on R1's
tests. It is rated **Tier 2**, and it is designed to ship as a mode inside Alternative A, which is
**Tier 3**.

**The ruling.** A mode cannot ship ahead of the card that carries it. lq-connect B is **Tier 1 on its
own merits and Tier 3 as shipped**, and the summary table records the host card's tier. The same logic
holds in the pipeline half, where sigpack 2A and 2B are correctly kept as separate designs rather than
modes precisely because their tiers differ.

**The consequence the maintainer should see.** Under Appetite B, lq-connect does not ship at all —
including the bring-your-own-directory mode, which on its own is the cleanest design in the companion
half. If the maintainer wants that capability under a Tiers 1–2 appetite, B has to be carved out as
its own card with its own description ("match a list you bring against a practice taxonomy"), which is
a real option and costs one bundle slot in each bundle.

### R6 — Tier 2 means one named probe; a probe set ranks last

**The drift.** Tier 2 is defined as "ships after **one** named probe passes". **sigpack 2B** is rated
Tier 2 conditional on **P4 and P8 both** passing, plus P2 or P9 for the ledger — three probes, and the
analyst's own commentary says so: "Three probes for one skill is the signal to build 2A first."

**The ruling.** A design gated on a probe *set* stays in Tier 2 but is recorded as **Tier 2 (probe
set)** and ranks behind every single-probe Tier 2 in the build order. It does not drop to Tier 3,
because nothing about its promise has changed — it is the full skill, waiting on evidence. sigpack 2B
is the only instance in the fourteen. Its fidelity line is also conditional in a way no other design's
is ("Core promise not deliverable **if P4 fails**"), and the honest reading of that pair is that it
should be re-rated the moment P4 returns, in either direction.

### R7 — One vocabulary rule, covering both halves

**The convergence.** Both halves independently arrived at a forbidden-words rule, for the same reason
and in different words. The pipeline half: shipped skills stop saying **receipt, certified, proved**
(and **complete**) where the arithmetic is self-performed. The companion half: a dated library is
forbidden from saying **currently** or **the community now thinks**.

**The ruling: one rule with two limbs, applied to all fourteen cards.**

- **Limb 1 — claims about coverage.** Where a count or a completeness claim is produced by the skill's
  own reading rather than by something the lawyer can check independently, the words *receipt*,
  *certified*, *proved* and *complete* may not be used. The permitted form names the method and the
  scope: "no issues found in the 62 clauses I read", never "no issues found".
- **Limb 2 — claims about currency.** Where an answer rests on a dated snapshot rather than on a live
  source, the words *currently*, *now*, *the latest* and *up to date* may not be used, and the
  snapshot's date appears in the description, the first line and the closing scope statement.

This is maintainer decision 2, widened. It should be settled before build 1, because it is cheap to
adopt and expensive to retrofit, and it should be swept across the shipped tree as well as the new
cards.

docreview, so the rewrite is done once and used twice, which is the argument for treating these two
as one piece of work.

### 8. Bundle and routing

Transactional, which has room. The two "out of scope" clauses that currently reserve this ground —
organize-case-docs' "document-by-document responsiveness and privilege review, which is out of scope"
and document-discovery's "reviewing an incoming production document by document, which is out of
scope" — sit in the litigation bundle. Because a skill may hand off by name only to skills present in
every bundle it ships in, a transactional diligence cannot name them; those two need a scope statement
rather than a hand-off, and diligence needs one against playbook-review.

### 9. The honest counter

"Continue inside the same skill. Do not replace the workflow with an informal whole-room review" (Q8)
is the sentence the alternative most has to honour, and the tranche design is how: the method stays,
the assurance drops, and nothing becomes "read the room and tell me what you think". "The fallback is
method-compatible, not assurance-equivalent. State exactly which checks were unavailable" (Q9) is the
required disclosure and belongs on the artifact. "A claim without a verified verbatim quote does not
enter any artifact" (Q11) survives in form — every row carries its quote and locator — and weakens in
verification, because the check is self-performed. "Never transmit anything anywhere" (Q5) is
satisfied: a skills-only package has no network channel of its own.

### 10. What the lawyer actually gets

They give the skill their issues list and the first tranche of the data room. They approve the
framework, look at five sample documents' findings and say what is wrong with them, and then the
skill works through the tranche, one document at a time, filling a workbook: document, issue, finding,
the verbatim words it relies on, where they are. Next session they attach the workbook and the next
tranche. At the end they have a crosswalk they can read and a workbook they can sort, with a visible
count of what was covered and a visible list of what was parked. What they never get is the sentence
saying the review is coverage-certified, and the skill says so at the start, not at the end.

## The lq-mirror surgery, as the template

The seventh companion skill, `lq-mirror`, already shipped. It is the worked example: the same surgery
this part designs five more times was performed on it once, in public, and the card is in the tree at
`skills/lq-mirror/skill.yaml`. Four moves, all visible there, recur in every alternative below.

1. **Delete the first-run marker section outright.** `sections: - match: '## First run? One marker,

**Capability risk: Low.** Every load-bearing step is D. Step 2b is a convenience U; step 5's lost
verification is a limit the skill already has words for. **[reconciled]** Step 4's folder U does not
move the rating, because the attach path is D and the folder was never load-bearing.

**Fidelity: Different deliverable.** Upstream's artifact exists to be submitted: "the real LQ
application, which is LQ Assess: a short form at https://assess.legalquants.com and then a 90-minute
observed work session; the assessment is the application" (Q10), closing on "the application is LQ
Assess... when they are ready; the public profile lives on legalquants.com once it is real" (Q11).
Remove the destination and what remains is an evidence-based professional profile with an honest gap
account. That is less than upstream, and it is not nothing: the discipline — declared kept apart from
inferred, attribution separated, "Gaps are asked, never filled — not even when they ask for 'polish' or
say everyone exaggerates", no verdict language anywhere — is the part a lawyer cannot get from a
generic CV drafter, and it ports intact.

**Failure modes.** Step 2b (U): **Silent** — a thin draft that looks like a complete one because the
evidence file was not found. **Mitigation:** the skill names the file it looked for, and the inventory
step (7) already shows what it read and what remains uncovered, which converts this to loud if kept.
**[ruling R3]** Continuity, not a hold: named and continued. Step 5: **Silent** if the model describes
a public profile it did not read. **Mitigation:** the skill's own rule — never attribute work without
confirming it belongs to them — plus an explicit "reported, not verified" label wherever a handle is
used.

**Tier 3**, conditions: (a) the destination and both URLs go; (b) the first line says it is not
connected to any application or assessment, so the changed promise is visible before the draft, not
after; (c) the collision with `writing` is resolved in the description; (d) the gap discipline is kept
word for word, because it is the reason to ship it at all. **A Tier 2 variant** exists if P2 passes:
the evidence file is read back unprompted and the skill becomes a running record rather than a one-off
draft. Build the Tier 3 version first; P2 improves it without changing its card. **[ruling R1]** Note
that the Tier 3 rating comes from test 1 — "Different deliverable" — and not from the capability
picture, which is Low and clean.

**Probes.** P2 for step 2b. **P12** bears on step 5 but nothing waits on it, and its availability arm
(an admin toggle that is off by default) is a second reason no design should.

**Effort and companion count.** 4 files today, no scripts of its own, **1 companion**
(`references/formats.md`, 2.9 KB) which needs a rewrite to drop the application slug mapping. The card
is a handful of `replace` rules plus the marker deletion — a small surgery. The hard part is the
description, which has to distinguish "arrange my evidence of working well with AI" from `writing`'s
"draft this document for me" without naming a product.

**Bundle and routing collisions.** Both bundles. `lq-mirror` on self-assessment — resolvable, since
lq-mirror gives a reading and this gives an artifact — and `writing` on "draft my CV", which is the
one that needs an explicit hand-off in both descriptions.

**The honest counter.** Q5 — "Never used: raw transcripts, unconfirmed or proposed items, anything that
looks like a client, a matter, or document content" — is honoured trivially. Q8's rule that writes go
only through the save door becomes "writes nothing at all", which is stricter. Q9 is the alternative's
own foundation and upstream calls it a normal mode, not a degradation. What the alternative cannot
honour is Q10 and Q11, and it should not try to imitate them with a vaguer door.

**Policy block: yes**, and decisive for the format — `assess.legalquants.com` and `legalquants.com`
under section 8. The capability picture is nearly clean; the purpose is what the policy removes.
Maintainer decision 1(c).

**What the lawyer actually gets.** They say "help me write up what I've actually done with AI". The
skill asks what to draw on, in order, starting with anything they have kept and ending with just
talking to it, and says at the top that it is not connected to any application or assessment. It shows
an inventory of what it read before it writes anything, asks one or two questions about work it
suspects is missing, and drafts a one-page legal-AI CV or a profile in which every line is traceable to
something they confirmed. Then it shows the gaps as a short list, in plain words, and refuses to fill
them however they ask. It saves the draft as a named file and keeps the private evidence notes in a
second one. Nothing is submitted, and it says so.

**Capability risk: Medium.** Two U/C steps. Step 10's bundled binary asset is U and has a documented
fallback in the same step, so it is not fatal. Step 4's tool-and-skill-event evidence is C and is the
one that decides whether this skill is useful: if Cowork exposes little of its own session to a skill,
the rubric's honest answer is *insufficient evidence*, every time. The skill would be loudly useless
rather than quietly wrong — which is the right failure, and still a failure. **P11** settles it.

**Fidelity: Same deliverable with a disclosed weaker guarantee.** The receipt, the judgement, the
rubric, the post and the confidentiality pass are untouched. The cover becomes an HTML card or a slide
the lawyer exports, and nothing is kept afterwards. Upstream's own text authorises the conversion path
(Q9) and forbids the tempting shortcut in the same breath — "Do not draw anything yourself, ask an
image model, or claim a PNG exists" — so the adaptation is inside the skill's own rules, not around
them.

**Failure modes.**
- Step 4 (C): **Loud**, because of the insufficient-evidence rule in §1 "Consent and scope" — "If the
  host cannot supply real evidence (some hosts expose little), say so plainly: that is an automatic
  *insufficient evidence* outcome, and it ends in refusal, not in a lower bar" — which is already
  written and must be kept verbatim. **[ruling R4]** This is the reference case for a self-applied gate
  that is genuinely Loud: the gate's only possible outcome is a refusal the lawyer reads. Without it
  this would be the most dangerous step in the companion half: a moment awarded on a session the skill
  could not actually see.
- Step 10 (U): **Loud** — a missing image in a preview pane is visible. **Mitigation:** the skill says
  which form of the cover it produced.

- Step 15: no failure — it writes nothing.

would ship now with the degrade and treat the template as an upgrade, because the cover's value is the
two sentences, not the background.

**Probes.** **P7b** (does a bundled binary companion reach an output file), **P11** (what a skill can
see and quote of its own session). **P7a** is the adjacent check on the shipped `legaldesign` and
`cite-check` templates and is worth running first because it is free — those skills already ship.
**[reconciled]** P7a and P7b are two arms of one probe in the unified numbering, which is also the
right way to run them: one package, one tester, two recorded results, no risk of the same failure
being attributed to two different causes.

**Effort and companion count.** The surgery the rebucket sketched, and it is accurate: `exclude:
scripts/**`; a `sections` overlay on step 3 replacing the render command with "fill the supplied cover
template with the two approved sentences and save it as an HTML page for the preview pane" — the
pattern `cite-check` uses with its report template and `legaldesign` uses throughout; a `replace`
deleting step 5's store write; a `replace` on the ending. Watch `LQC-W009`: "the renderer" and
"subprocess" are host words and must not survive in any `*.md`. 10 files today, **6 companions** after
the strip (`copy.md`, `cover.md`, `examples.md`, `rubric.md`, `assets/README.md`, and
`assets/cover-template.png` at 583 KB) — 6 of the documented 20-file cap, and 602 KB of the documented
10 MB total (C1#17). **[reconciled]** The earlier note of a "5 MB per-file cap" has no support in the
reader's extraction — the documented figures are 50 custom skills, 1 MB per `SKILL.md`, 20 companion
files and 10 MB total per skill — so the headroom is larger than stated, though the conclusion is
unchanged. `cover.md` needs a rewrite for the HTML form. If P7b fails, the template and its README go
and it is **4 companions**.

**Bundle and routing collisions.** Both bundles, as a core-adjacent reflective skill. `lq-mirror`
already claims "assess me", "what's my archetype" and "where am I with AI"; this one's ask is narrower
("was this session the good kind?") and the two descriptions need an explicit hand-off in both
directions. `legaldesign` owns "make me a polished page for the preview pane" and would otherwise take
the cover request — the cover must be described as part of this skill's output, never as a design job.

**The honest counter.** Q2 and Q7 forbid reading history, the profile or a playbook, and the
alternative reads none of them. Q3's "never by an image model" and Q9's three prohibitions survive
unchanged and must stay in the body. Q5's "Never post, transmit, schedule, queue, or invoke a
publishing connector, even when asked" survives and is now also structurally true. What is lost, and
what the card's `notes` must say: the rasterised PNG, the fixed template if P7b fails, and the kept
line — so a moment becomes a reading, not a record.

**Policy block:** none. my-lq-moment is the only one of the six with no external URL anywhere in its
own text. There is a judgement call that is not a policy block: "My LQ Moment" is a LegalQuants
construct and the rubric judges against that programme's standard, in a bundle installed by tenants who
are not members. The skill's own rules already forbid implying membership or certification; the
maintainer should decide whether that is enough — maintainer decision 7.

**What the lawyer actually gets.** They finish something that went unusually well and say "was that
actually impressive or am I flattering myself?" The skill explains what it is about to do, asks
permission to look at this session's work, and then either says plainly that this is not an earned
moment and why — or gives them a private receipt: what the situation was, what they did, what came
back, what they checked, what to take from it, with every claim tied to something in the session. If
they want it, they get a LinkedIn post in their own voice and a cover card that opens in the preview
pane, with two short sentences on it, which they can export as an image themselves. It never posts
anything, never saves anything, and if the session does not give it enough to go on it says so and
stops rather than lowering the bar.

## One thing worth saying to the maintainer

Nine of these fourteen skills already contain their own Cowork-native version, written by their own
authors as the branch for a host that could not do the full thing — lq-ask Q5, lq-apply Q9, lq-reflect
Q9, my-lq-moment Q9 and its insufficient-evidence rule, read-redline Q6, definition-check Q6, diligence
Q10, regulatory Q9. In every case that branch is closer to what Cowork can do than anything an adapter
would invent, and shipping it costs no new capability claim and no new promise. The design work in this
document is mostly the work of deciding whether the branch, standing alone, is still a skill worth
having — and of writing the one sentence that tells the lawyer which branch they are in.

The reconciliation changed the picture in three places worth naming on their own. **Editing an existing
document is documented**, which removes the biggest single U from four register-based designs and turns
P9 from a question about whether the pattern works into a question about how faithfully it works.
**Browser use is a documented route** rather than an unknown one — though it is generally available
and *disabled by default*, so the original "not in any given tenant" reading stands and two designs
keep the caveat they were written with; no tier moved in either direction, because nothing in the
fourteen has a load-bearing step on the browser. And **"Deep Research" is the current name** of the built-in that several of these
skills must route around, so the shipped tree and CONTRACT section 8 are right as they stand and no
rename sweep is needed.
